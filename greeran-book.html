I
Editorial premise

How to make a balanced book from an uneven record

The public record around Steven Thomas Greeran is uneven. Officially, the strongest and clearest documentation concerns a California conviction later referenced by Virgin Islands authorities, his registration history in the U.S. Virgin Islands, and the 2025 charge for alleged registration noncompliance.12 Separately, a self-published web resume supplies a much broader but less authoritative map of schooling, work, and volunteer activity across Southern California and Santa Barbara County.9

A third and fourth body of material now joins the file. A subject-supplied family-history research record — the Seize Quartiers genealogy file and an interactive military-service map — extends the book backward across seven generations and two countries, from Drumgooland in County Down to Revolutionary Pennsylvania.1011 And a growing shelf of self-published standalone HTML5 tools extends it forward, documenting a working cryptography, censorship-analysis, audio-DSP, naturalist, and genealogy toolset of the 2020s — including one artifact that presents the resume itself as an RC4-encrypted puzzle.122026 Both arrive labeled as self-published or research-file material, and both are kept on their own rung of the evidence ladder.

This book model treats those two evidence classes differently. Official records anchor the legal chronology. Resume material fills out the earlier life-and-work trail without pretending to know motives, feelings, or unwritten episodes. The result is not a traditional memoir, but it is a more balanced timeline than a dossier limited only to the 2025 case.9

The point of this edition is not to romanticize or to flatten the record. It is to keep the whole available timeline in view while labeling what is official, what is self-published, and what remains unverified.

Growing up at 2860, the Landers soccer team, and Seller's violin

⚠ Personal recollection — not independently verified

The house was at 2860 in Glendora. The room was small and in the back — a kid's room, the kind of space where the world gets built from whatever is within arm's reach. Soccer came first among organized activities. The team was the Landers, and the position was halfback. At school — likely linked to the Glendora Unified School District — students were required to learn a musical instrument. The instrument chosen was violin, and the place was Seller's. Family connections reached into the school district itself. Aunt Lila — wife of Uncle Chuck — worked as Dr. Bushman's secretary at the district office of the Glendora Unified School District. Emergency! — the real connection

Emergency! premiered on NBC on January 15, 1972 and ran 124 episodes through 1977. It was the last show created by Jack Webb (Dragnet, Adam-12). The fictional "Station 51" was actually Fire Station 127 at 2049 East 223rd Street in Carson, California, chosen for its natural lighting by co-creator Robert Cinader. The real dispatcher heard on the show was Sam Lanier, an actual LACoFD dispatcher with over 18 years' service. The character of John Gage was based on Battalion Chief Jim Page, who helped create the LACoFD paramedic program.

When the show debuted, only 12 paramedic squads existed in the entire United States. By the time it ended first-run production, more than half of all Americans were within 4–7 minutes of advanced life-saving care. The show was one of the most successful public health education campaigns in American history.

"KMG365" — the call sign Station 51's crew uses to acknowledge dispatch — is a real FCC station license assigned to LACoFD Fire Station 98 in Bellflower. It appears on the station patch for Station 127.

Maurice had a workbench at his house in Duarte — not a shop, but the place where the user used to hang out and learn soldering. Maurice Frederick Hewitt — ham radio callsign KG6ZD — was another son of Robert. The ham radio world was its own kind of fire service: a network of voices on the air, people listening and responding, a community built around the ability to reach out across distance with nothing but a signal and a frequency.

Chantry Flat — Big Santa Anita Canyon

The family's mountain connection traces to Chantry Flat in the Big Santa Anita Canyon, Angeles National Forest. Originally known as "Poison Oak Flat," it was renamed after prospector Charley Chantry, who arrived in 1905. During the Great Hiking Era (1890s–1920s), over 220 cabins were built in the canyon. Materials were hauled in by mules and people's backs on narrow mountain trails — pack trains traveled seven days a week during the busy construction years. The Chantry Road to the trailhead wasn't built until 1935; before that, you hiked from Sierra Madre, adding four miles each way.

Great-grandfather brought the family up to Angeles Crest campground on top of the ridge, where they built huts — materials packed up by mule from Chantry Flats. They lived in cabins at Chantry Flats. Today about 80 cabins remain, privately owned on USFS land leases, with no electricity or utilities — everything still brought in by mule. The canyon has the last magneto-type crank phone system in the United States. Adams Pack Station, operating since 1936, is the last remaining pack station in Southern California — and it's where mail and supplies come from. Sturtevant Camp, founded in 1893, is one of the oldest resorts in the San Gabriels.

The cabins have survived the 1938 flood, the 1953 Monrovia Peak Fire, the 1969 flood, and the 2020 Bobcat Fire (which destroyed 18 of the 80 remaining cabins). They cannot be rebuilt if lost — a living museum to early 20th-century Southern California mountain life.

Nike missile sites — the San Gabriel ridge defense perimeter

During the Cold War, 16 Nike missile sites surrounded Los Angeles in the "Ring of Supersonic Steel" — designed to intercept Soviet bombers before they could reach the city. The San Gabriel Mountains ridge was lined with missile sites, designed to lay cover for Los Angeles to retreat — a defensive perimeter built into the mountain skyline.

Aerojet — Grandfather Bernard in Azusa

Grandfather Bernard worked at Aerojet in Azusa, California — the rocket engine manufacturing facility at 1100 W Hollyvale St, Azusa, CA 91702. Aerojet was founded in 1942 and moved to Azusa in January 1943, producing JATO (Jet-Assisted TakeOff) units and rocket engines for the military. Bernard had security clearance — classified defense work during the Cold War.

Aerojet sold the Azusa facility in 2001 to Northrop Grumman Corporation, which now operates it as part of its Space Systems division. The facility transitioned from rocket production into R&D, optics, and infrared detectors for satellite-based projects.

The Azusa site was declared an EPA Superfund Site in 1985 (San Gabriel Superfund Site II) after TCE contamination was discovered in groundwater in 1980. In 1997, NDMA and ammonium perchlorate contamination were also found. Cleanup continues under the Baldwin Park Operable Unit.

Aerojet's environmental footprint extends across multiple states: the Rancho Cordova facility near Sacramento (8,500 acres, Superfund since 1983, 27-square-mile groundwater contamination plume); Chino Hills (800-acre ordnance facility, munitions and depleted uranium cleanup); and Homestead, Florida — near the Everglades, where a 1963 rocket test went wrong: the nozzle ejected, spreading hydrochloric acid across wetlands, crop fields, and homes. The massive AJ-260-2 rocket motor still sits in its silo today.

JPL, Caltech & the Aerojet connection

Aerojet and JPL were born from the same group. In 1936, Caltech graduate student Frank Malina and self-taught rocket enthusiasts Jack Parsons and Edward Forman — the "Suicide Squad" — tested their first rocket motor in the Arroyo Seco on Halloween 1936. Their advisor, Theodore von Kármán, secured Army funding for JATO (Jet-Assisted Take-Off) rockets.

In 1943, Parsons, Malina, Forman, and von Kármán established the Aerojet Engineering Corporation to manufacture JATO motors. JPL was officially named in November 1943. Aerojet moved to Azusa; JPL stayed in Pasadena. Bernard worked in the same building where Caltech/JPL scientists worked.

Morris Dam — torpedo testing in the San Gabriel Mountains

Just a few miles north of Azusa, the same Caltech/JPL scientists ran the Morris Reservoir Naval Weapons Test Site — a torpedo testing facility at Morris Dam on the San Gabriel River. Built in 1934 by Pasadena for water supply, the 245-foot concrete dam impounds a 1.5-mile-long, 160-foot-deep reservoir.

In 1943, Caltech established the test site for the U.S. Navy as a substation of the Naval Ordnance Test Station at China Lake. Caltech managed operations from 1943 to 1950. Testing focused on torpedoes, depth bombs, and submarine-launched weapons — including the Polaris missile. The Variable Angle Launcher (VAL) was the only such structure in the nation: a 300-foot welded steel frame firing torpedoes at up to 680 mph into the reservoir, adjustable from 5 to 38 degrees entry angle.

Caltech graduate student Conway Snyder — later a JPL scientist who spent 30 years on lunar and interplanetary exploration — accidentally launched the world's first underwater rocket from a rowboat at Morris Dam. Testing whether rockets would produce bubble trails underwater, the sixth rocket shot out of the water and through the air. This mishap helped lead to the development of the submarine-launched Polaris ICBM.

The torpedo tests "paid off for the fleet with the tremendous victories won by Navy aviators at the Battle of Leyte Gulf in October 1944" — 60 enemy ships sunk. Testing continued until June 1993, decommissioned under BRAC in 1997. The concrete ski-jump launch ramp is still visible from Highway 39. In October 2024, Morris Dam was designated a National Historic Engineering Landmark on its 90th anniversary.

Self-published works · a motif and what it became

The ensō line: a brushstroke that became a key

◆ Self-published works — author-attributed. The artifacts are citable; the reading of them below is editorial, not a sourced claim about the subject's beliefs.

Five artifacts supplied with the fifth resource batch share one figure. The ensō — the circle brushed in a single stroke in Zen calligraphy, traditionally left open, the gap part of the form rather than a flaw in it — appears first as a drawing and ends as a key.5455565758

The progression is visible build by build. The first generator draws a circle from a seed ID and exports it as a PNG; a "random terrain" control sets the same stroke against a generated landscape.54 The second build adds one field — custom seed (optional) — and with it the ensō stops being only an image: any phrase can now determine the circle.55 That is the hinge of the whole line.

The next three builds walk through the opened door. BIP-39 Haiku Wallet & Ensō Forge mines for a seed phrase that is simultaneously a checksum-valid BIP-39 mnemonic and a metrically exact 5-7-5 haiku, and draws the ensō from the same parameters — "mining ensures exact 5-7-5 syllables with a valid BIP39 SHA-256 checksum."56 Ensō Haiku Wallet Forge instruments every variable — haiku, mnemonic, BIP-44 path, xpub, P2PKH address, SHA-256 wallet fingerprint, ensō parameters — and collapses the whole set into a single printable Ensō Reference Number, base-62 encoded: "unique seed encoding all parameters." The circle becomes the record.57 The last build makes the line offline: rather than fetching the wordlist, the user pastes the official 2048 words once and they are "permanently committed to your browser's local sandbox storage so that all cryptographic operations can run fully offline," behind an AES-256-GCM vault and a batch miner with a grammar filter that "automatically skips awkward poetic line breaks."58

Trade-show floors · personal recollection, venue-verified

The convention floor: Black Hat, CES, and the halls that date them

◆ Personal recollection — attendance is unverified. The buildings, shows, dates and name changes below are sourced; the presence of any person in them is not.

A separate thread of the record is not documentary at all. It is a set of remembered rooms: a rooftop lounge on a 64th floor, a convention hall stacked directly above another convention hall, a west-coast exhibition floor behind Disneyland, a New York shed on Eleventh Avenue. The book cannot verify that the subject stood in any of them. What it can do — and what the research pass of 28 September 2026 did — is verify the rooms themselves, because convention venues are unusually well dated. Halls open on a known day, shows move between them in a known year, and buildings are renamed on a known date. A memory that names a venue is therefore a memory that can be bracketed in time, even when nothing else about it can be confirmed.75

◆ August in Las Vegas — Black Hat, Mandalay Bay, and the 64th floor

The Las Vegas convention was Black Hat, with an evening meeting at Skyfall, the lounge on the 64th floor of the Delano. Those two details sit naturally together: Black Hat USA has been held at the Mandalay Bay Convention Center every year since 2014, having run at Caesars Palace from 2002 through 2013, and the Delano tower is part of the same Mandalay Bay complex — a conference day and a rooftop meeting are a walk across one property, not a trip up the Strip.76 The convention center itself had just been enlarged: a $70 million expansion completed across 2014–16 took it to roughly 2.1 million square feet.77

The tower has had three names in twenty years — THEhotel at Mandalay Bay from its opening on 17 December 2003, Delano Las Vegas from the ribbon cutting of 2 September 2014, and W Las Vegas since 18 December 2024 — and Skyfall itself, with Alain Ducasse's Rivea beside it, did not open on the 64th floor until October 2015.78 That last date is the useful one. Black Hat USA 2015 finished on 6 August, two months before the lounge existed. A Black Hat trip that included an evening at Skyfall therefore falls in 2016 or later — and not in 2020, when the conference was held virtually.

The recollection also places the Las Vegas Convention Center in the picture. In Black Hat week that means DEF CON, which moved to the LVCC West Hall for DEF CON 32 on 8–11 August 2024 after Caesars cancelled its contract, and returned there for DEF CON 33 on 7–10 August 2025.79 If the Mandalay Bay floor, the Skyfall evening and the LVCC all belong to one week, that week was August 2024 or August 2025. It is the tightest bracket in this chapter, and it was produced entirely from venue history — no private record was needed to reach it, and none has been used to assert it.

◆ January in Las Vegas — a hall above a hall

A second Las Vegas memory belongs to a different decade. It is of CES running above the AVN Adult Entertainment Expo in the Sands Expo hall — and the building really is arranged that way. The Sands Expo stacks its exhibit space: halls A, B, C and D sit on the upper level under roughly 32½-foot ceilings, while Hall G is the lower hall, about 380,000 square feet under a ceiling of 13 feet 5 inches.80 The adult expo began, as the Las Vegas Sun put it in 2012, "in the basement of the Sands Expo and Convention Center in the 1980s," and ran there in the same week as CES until the two shows separated: in 2012 AEE moved to the following weekend and to the Hard Rock Hotel, and the shows did not share a week again until 2023, by which time they were in different buildings a mile apart.81

So a visit that found one show physically above the other happened in January 2011 or earlier, in a building then called the Sands Expo — it only became the Venetian Expo on 2 September 2021, during the sale of the Venetian–Palazzo complex.82 That is five years or more before the earliest possible Skyfall evening. These are two separate trips, and this book prints them as two. The nearby Renaissance Las Vegas — 548 rooms over fifteen floors, non-gaming and smoke-free, adjacent to the Convention Center on Paradise Road — fits either era, having opened in 2004; it is the choice of someone working a show floor rather than a casino.83

◆ Anaheim and New York — the MD&M pair

MD&M West is held at the Anaheim Convention Center, which opened in 1967 with the domed Arena on Katella Avenue and has been expanded seven times since. The expansion that matters for dating is ACC North, opened 26 September 2017: 200,000 square feet on two levels, 100,000 of it column-free, with a balcony over Katella and a climate-controlled pedestrian bridge into the older halls. It took the complex to 1.8 million square feet, the largest convention center on the West Coast.84 Anyone who remembers crossing that bridge was there after September 2017; anyone who does not may have been there before it existed. The show itself is older than its present name: the MD&M portfolio dates from 1985, its 41st edition ran in February 2026, and the event was branded IME West until Informa unified its advanced-manufacturing shows under the MD&M name for the 2025 season.85

MD&M East is a different coast and a different rhythm: it runs at the Jacob K. Javits Convention Center in New York — 20–22 May 2025, next on 19–20 May 2027 — and alternates biennially with MD&M South, so there is no East show every year. Under the older IME East branding it sometimes ran in December instead, as it did on 7–9 December 2021.86 "East and West" is therefore not an annual pair, and the book should not imply that it was.

◆ The other convention center

One more convention center belongs in this chapter, and it has nothing to do with trade shows. Lingua, Jim Sanborn's 2002 work at the Walter E. Washington Convention Center in Washington, D.C., is two sixteen-foot bronze cylinders, waterjet-cut with historical texts in eight languages — Russian, Chinese, Ethiopic, French, Spanish, Latin, Greek and Iroquois, some of them reaching back some three and a half thousand years, the Chinese section reproducing Wang Xizhi's Lantingji Xu — and lit from within, so the writing is thrown across the building's surfaces.87 That is the convention center the workshop already had. The remembered “convention center VRML” is not a lost file and not a trade-show model: it is the Sanborn installation viewer in the book lab, the WebGL app that carries eleven Sanborn sites — Kryptos and its entrance slabs, the Berlin Wall segments, the Cyrillic Projector, Coastline, Indian Run, Antipodes, Atomic Time, the cryptologic museum, Find the Lodestone, and Lingua — and lets a reader step between them with the number keys.

Convention Center”: the building opened the year after the sculpture was installed and was renamed for the District's first Home Rule mayor in 2007, so the plaque needed both the current name and the sequence. ◆ The yellow books

One memory of these floors is easy to check and worth checking, because it is half wrong in a way that matters. The recollection is of a For Dummies guide to the show — and of those yellow-and-black books being handed out at the convention center and being a hit. There is no Consumer Electronics Show For Dummies. There is no RSA For Dummies either. The author's own library list, a thousand titles deep, runs straight from Consulting to Consumer Behavior to Container Gardening, and from Reverse Mortgages to RFID to RTLS.88

The books on the floor were real, though, and the catalogue proves it. Wiley sells sponsored For Dummies, [Sponsor] Special Edition booklets as content marketing: written to order, the sponsor's name on the cover, forty-odd stapled pages, no retail edition and no cover price, given away at the booth and gated as a PDF afterwards. They are not informal handouts — they carry ISBNs and library records. A catalogue search returns a hundred and twenty-eight of them, from SIP Trunking For Dummies, Sonus Special Edition in 2012 to Zero Trust Security For Dummies, Edgewise Special Edition in 2018, nearly all John Wiley & Sons.89

And CES has its own. No sponsor ever put the show's name on a cover, so there is no CES For Dummies to find; what there is, is the show's exhibitors commissioning the books and timing them to the show. Qorvo — the RF-chip company whose wireless unit sells into the smart home — published Internet of Things For Dummies with Wiley on 5 December 2016, in two volumes, free, one month before CES 2017: smart-home market opportunity, IoT communications standards, security. A later second edition added Matter. Beside it sit IoT Solutions For Dummies, ARM Special Edition, Wi-Fi 6 For Dummies, Extreme Networks Special Edition, and, from the consumer-electronics retail side, Windows XP For Dummies, Limited Edition, a Circuit City custom book from 2003.91 On the RSA side the record is doubled: the conference's own library reviews Cryptography For Dummies and Hacking For Dummies — the same yellow books its sponsors were commissioning.92

So the memory is sound and only the title was wrong. What it describes is a sponsored special edition from an exhibitor's booth — a real, catalogued book with an ISBN that was never sold — not a guidebook to the show.

Two smaller things fall out of the same check. The library is a dated record of what the author was reading before any of this: Cryptography For Dummies and Cracking Codes and Cryptograms For Dummies sit in it, a decade before the Sanborn apps in Chapter IX.88 And the Internet Archive holds no CES guidebook at all — what it holds instead are Mattel's internal 1978 CES planning memos and Commodore's 1984 Winter CES preparation file, which are better documents than the guidebook would have been.90 The “pub crawl” remains unverified and stays out of the record until something dated turns up.

All six buildings are now modelled as schematic exteriors in the book lab's venue exhibit, with the research and every citation kept beside them.75 The models are interpretive massing — invented footprints, invented spacing, no interiors — with three sourced measurements: the Sands hall split, Skyfall's storey, and Lingua's sixteen feet.
