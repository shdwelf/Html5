## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Alfred Houle of Biddeford — corroborated in a primary source ✅

The **Biddeford and Saco Directory 1920–1921** is digitised, free, and full-text searchable on
the Internet Archive — item `biddeford-and-saco-directory-1920-1921`, uploaded by the Dyer
Library / Saco Museum (`abrown@dyerlibrarysacomuseum.org`), Public Domain Mark 1.0, 381 scanned
pages, OCR by tesseract 5.3.0, published 2026-05-11.

Two independent hits inside that one volume:

- **Surname listing (scanned page 129):**
  `Houle Alfred policeman h 12 George`
- **Biddeford Police Department roster (scanned page 264):**
  "Chief, Thomas Stone; captain, Ernest H. Robbins; regular officers, Arthur Sabourin, George F.
  Frye, Marcel Metayer, Florence Driscoll, Joseph Goulette, Joseph Langelier, **Alfred Houle**,
  Henry A. Descoteaux, Roy Foss."
- Same page 129, consistent household: `Houle Edna Miss operative PMCo bds 12 George` — a young
  woman boarding at Alfred's address, the pattern a directory uses for a daughter at home.

**What this proves:** an Alfred Houle lived in Biddeford in 1920–21 at 12 George Street and was a
sworn regular officer of the city police. Existence, residence, and occupation are now backed by
a primary source rather than an uncited visualization.

**What it does not prove:** parentage (a directory never gives it), and no wife. Directories of
this period list heads of household only, so **Eugénie Tremblay** is neither confirmed nor
excluded by this volume. Slot 22's *surname and town* are corroborated; the *couple* is not.

Other Biddeford Houles in the same listing, for disambiguation: Augusta (Mrs) h 4 Bradbury;
Damase, farmer, h 252 Pool rd; Frank, millhand PMCo, h 154 Cleaves; Henry, truckman, 73 Water;
Henry jr, driver, bds 73 Water; John, weaver PMCo, h 20 King; Joseph, millhand, h 146 Cleaves;
Lillian, clerk 259 Main, bds 4 Bradbury; Louise (Miss), operative YMCo, bds 73 Water; Saul
(Garon & Houle, grocers, 53 Bacon) h 329 Main; Mary (Mrs) h 65 Water; Annette, operative YMCo,
bds 65 Water; Richard, mill operative, bds 65 Water.

### 2.2 The Morin surname in Biddeford — abundant, therefore not self-identifying ⚠️

The same volume's Morin listing runs from scanned page 162 (cross-reference `MORAN SEE MORIN`)
across page 163 and beyond — roughly **thirty Morin entries**, including **three different men
named "Morin Frank"**:

- `Morin Frank marble worker (Portland) h 53 Birch`
- `Morin Frank quarryman h 31 Adams`
- `Morin Frank weaver h 29 Pine`

plus Morin Adelard (slasher LMCo, bds 21 Lambert); Aglaee (Miss, bds 20 Beacon av); Albert J
(clerk 27 Water, bds 2 Pearl); Alfred (operative PMCo, h 4 Sullivan); Alfred T (clerk 201 Main,
h at Saco); two Alphonse; Armand; Blanche; David; Emile; Emma (Mrs); Ernest; Ferdinand; George
(baker, 20 Beacon av); George E; Henry (barber, Saco); Jerome A (manager, 259-261 Main, h 17
Cutts); John (boxworker); John B (president, 259-261 Main, h 17 Cutts); two Joseph; Joseph U;
Josephine; Laura; two Louis; Philip; Pierre; Rene; two Rosanna; Rose — and the **Morin Drug Co.**
at 259-261 Main, whose front-cover advertisement appears throughout the volume. A South Portland
monument firm, Ballantine & Morin, also advertises heavily.

**Interpretation.** This is a useful negative. "François Morin of Biddeford" cannot be promoted
from `src-45` on directory evidence, because in 1920–21 Biddeford the name resolves to at least
three working men. The directory establishes that the family name was dense in exactly the town
the workbench names — which is consistent with `src-45` and inconsistent with a fabricated slot —
but identity requires a record that carries a wife's name: a marriage record, a death certificate,
or a directory widow entry.

### 2.3 A competing François Morin lead, and the certificate that would settle it 🟡

A publicly indexed community-tree aggregation returned a Biddeford candidate:

> **François Morin** — b. 29 Oct 1883, Saint-Jude, Québec; d. Mar 1963, **Biddeford, York, Maine**;
> father **François Morin**; mother **Célanire Couture**.

This is a *community tree*, the same evidentiary class as the FamilySearch PRF that `src-10`
leans on: it names parents, which is exactly what slot 22 needs, but it cites no certificate.
It also **conflicts with `src-45`** — the workbench puts François Morin's birth at ~1868, fifteen
years earlier, and places the family in Beauce rather than Saint-Jude (Montmagny region). Two
uncited claims disagreeing by fifteen years is precisely the situation Chapter XII says should be
resolved by documents rather than merged by preference.

**The named test:** a François Morin died at Biddeford in March 1963, which falls inside the
**Maine death index, 1960–1996**, indexed and searchable free on the Maine State Archives
website; the underlying certificate names the deceased's parents and birthplace. One index
lookup decides whether the community tree or the workbench survives, and either outcome promotes
slot 22 from evidenced to proven — or retires both claims.

### 2.4 Daisy Beatrice — the Find A Grave route is now closed ❌

`src-10` used Find A Grave to prove three other slots (Millie Ruth Bickford #181036078, Joel
Thornburg Carey #87170349, Elizabeth Stanley #87170242), so the same route was tried for Daisy.

- Query — first name *Daisy*, middle name *Beatrice*, surname *Carey*, death year *1989*, exact:
  **"No matches found."**
- Query broadened — first name *Daisy*, surname *Carey*, death year 1989 ±5 years: **one match**,
  *Mrs Daisy Hope Morgan Carey, 16 Feb 1920 – 26 Nov 1989*, Goulburn General Cemetery, Goulburn
  Mulwaree Council, New South Wales, Australia (memorial 201871127).

The single match is a **namesake trap** worth recording: right surname, right death year, wrong
continent and wrong birth year (1920, not 1904). Anyone searching loosely for "Daisy Carey d.
1989" will find her and could silently import an Australian family into a Texas line.

**Result:** slots 28–29 have no cemetery route. Only certificates remain, exactly as `src-10`
predicted: the Los Angeles County marriage of Francis Gray Carey × Daisy (~1921–22), a Texas
birth certificate (19 Sep 1904), or the California death certificate (20 Jan 1989, Monrovia).

### 2.5 No Maine Houle on Find A Grave ❌

Query — surname *Houle*, birth year 1870 ±10, death before 1940: **two matches, both in Canada.**
*Denise Houle* (1870–1940), Saint Victor Parish Cemetery, Alfred, Prescott and Russell United
Counties, Ontario (memorial 218342527); *Onesime Houle* (1870–1940), Cimetière Saint-Calixte,
Plessisville, Centre-du-Québec, Québec (memorial 233742939). No Biddeford gravestone for the
workbench's Alfred Houle — so his dates must come from the directory series and Maine vital
records, not from a memorial.

### Reproducing the directory searches

The Internet Archive volumes are full-text searchable without an account. Item metadata:
`https://archive.org/metadata/biddeford-and-saco-directory-1920-1921`. Search inside a volume
(the `doc` value is the item's own title, and `path` comes from the metadata `dir` field):

```
https://ia801900.us.archive.org/fulltext/inside.php
  ?item_id=biddeford-and-saco-directory-1920-1921
  &doc=Biddeford%20and%20Saco%20Directory%201920-1921
  &path=%2F17%2Fitems%2Fbiddeford-and-saco-directory-1920-1921
  &q=Houle
```

The endpoint is rate-limited and intermittently returns HTTP 502; retry after a pause. Returned
`page` numbers are **scanned leaf indices**, not the directory's printed page numbers — cite them
as "scanned page N" as this log does. Free sibling volumes in the same series: 1900, 1902, 1907,
1911, 1913, 1929–1930 (identifiers `biddeford-and-saco-directory-<year>`), plus the earlier
*Saco and Biddeford* runs from 1849, 1856–7, 1870, 1872–3, 1877, 1880, 1882, 1884, 1886, 1888,
1892–3, 1894, and 1896.
