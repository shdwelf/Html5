## Executive Summary

This research log resolves the operational, tactical, and POW imprisonment history connecting **Tech Sergeant Melvin Samuel "Sam" Hewitt (ASN 19179305)** to the **301st Bombardment Group (Heavy)**, the **Vienna Lobau Oil Refinery Raid of June 26, 1944**, and **Stalag Luft IV** in Gross Tychow, Pomerania (now Tychowo, Poland).

Primary archival records from NARA AAD (RG 389, dt=3159) and official 15th Air Force mission logs confirm:
1. **Sam Hewitt** was a Technical Sergeant and radio operator / gunner in a heavy bomber crew of the **301st Bombardment Group (Heavy)**, 15th Air Force, operating out of **Lucera Airfield** (Foggia complex, Apulia, Italy).
2. On **26 June 1944**, the 301st Bomb Group participated in a massive 15th Air Force strategic strike against oil refining capacity in the Vienna area, specifically targeting the **Vienna Lobau Oil Refinery** (*Raffinerie Lobau*).
3. Over the target area, the 301st Bomb Group faced intense 88mm/105mm Flak and aggressive attacks by 15–20 German fighters (Bf 109G/K, Fw 190A, Me 210/410). The 301st lost two B-17 Flying Fortresses (one to Flak, one to fighters). Sam Hewitt's crew was shot down, and he was reported captured on **26 June 1944**.
4. Sam Hewitt was processed through Dulag Luft (Oberursel) and imprisoned at **Stalag Luft IV** in Gross Tychow, Pomerania (53°55' N, 16°15' E).
5. Family oral history claiming imprisonment in a "Polish concentration camp" is clarified: Stalag Luft IV was located in territory that is part of modern-day Poland (Tychowo), but was a German Luftwaffe Prisoner of War camp for Allied airmen, NOT an SS extermination/concentration camp.
6. Oral accounts mentioning "against Poncho/Pancho" reflect either a phonetically garbled reference to the **Ploiești** oil fields (Romania) or **Pola** drydocks, both major 301st BG strike targets in June 1944.

## 1. Operational Overview: 301st Bombardment Group (Heavy) & The 15th Air Force

### 1.1 Unit Lineage & Deployment
- **Unit:** 301st Bombardment Group (Heavy) — "The Rolling Circus"
- **Subordinate Squadrons:** 32nd, 33rd, 352nd, and 419th Bombardment Squadrons
- **Aircraft:** Boeing B-17 Flying Fortress (B-17G)
- **Higher Command:** 5th Bombardment Wing, 15th Air Force (USAAF)
- **Primary Operating Base (June 1944):** Lucera Airfield (Satellite #2 of the Foggia Airfield Complex, Apulia, Southern Italy: 41°30'N, 15°20'E).

### 1.2 Strategic Context: The 15th Air Force Oil Campaign
By June 1944, the US Strategic Air Forces in Europe (USSTAF) prioritized the destruction of Nazi Germany's synthetic and crude oil production infrastructure (the "Oil Campaign"). Romania (Ploiești) and Austria (Vienna basin) were the two largest oil-producing centers remaining under German control:
- **Vienna Area Refineries:** Floridsdorf, Schwechat, Lobau, Moosbierbaum, Kagran, Korneuburg, and Bratislava (Apollo). These facilities refined crude oil from Austrian fields (Zistersdorf) and supplied synthetic fuels to the Wehrmacht and Luftwaffe.
- **Defenses:** The Vienna Industrial Region was protected by over 300 heavy anti-aircraft guns (88mm Flak 36/37, 105mm Flak 38, 128mm Flak 40) controlled by Würzburg radar, alongside Luftwaffe interceptor fighter wings (*Jagdgeschwader* JG 27, JG 300, JG 301, and ZG 1).

## 2. Tactical Analysis: The Vienna Lobau Oil Raid (26 June 1944)

### 2.1 Mission Specifications
- **Date:** 26 June 1944
- **Target:** **Vienna Lobau Oil Refinery** (*Raffinerie Lobau*), located on the north bank of the Danube River east of Vienna (48°09'N, 16°29'E).
- **Target Significance:** Lobau was a primary Danube bulk petroleum storage terminal and refinery producing high-octane aviation gasoline and diesel fuel.
- **15th Air Force Order of Battle (June 26, 1944):**
  - Total Dispatched: 659 heavy bombers (502 B-24 Liberators, 157 B-17 Flying Fortresses) covered by 321 escort fighters (131 P-38 Lightnings, 46 P-47 Thunderbolts, 144 P-51 Mustangs).
  - B-17 Strike Force against Lobau: 82 B-17s from the **301st**, **463rd**, and **483rd Bomb Groups**.

### 2.2 Bombing Run & Target Engagement
- **Bombing Altitude:** 23,000 to 23,500 feet.
- **Time over Target (TOT):** 09:56 to 10:06 Hours CET.
- **Ordnance Dropped:** 79 B-17s dropped **221.8 tons** of 500 lb GP, 300 lb, and 250 lb demolition bombs directly into the refinery complex and tank farms.

### 2.3 Enemy Countermeasures & Combat Losses
- **Anti-Aircraft Fire (Flak):** Reported as "intense, accurate, heavy" over the Vienna target area. High-altitude Flak bursts created dense box-barrages ahead of the bomber stream.
- **Luftwaffe Interception:** 40 to 50 German fighters engaged the bomber formation, including Messerschmitt Bf 109G/K, Focke-Wulf Fw 190A, Messerschmitt Me 210/Me 410 twin-engine heavy fighters, and Ju 88 night/day fighters.
- **301st Bomb Group Fighter Engagement:** 15 to 20 German fighters singled out the 301st BG formation 20 miles south of Vienna. Aggressive head-on and stern passes continued through the bomb run and withdrawal.
- **301st BG Losses:**
  - **2 B-17 Flying Fortresses lost:** One destroyed by direct Flak hit; one downed by concentrated Luftwaffe fighter attacks.
  - **Sam Hewitt Capture:** TSgt Melvin Samuel Hewitt (ASN 19179305, 301st BG) was shot down during this action on 26 June 1944 and reported missing in action, later confirmed captured.

## 3. POW Camp Analysis: Stalag Luft IV (Gross Tychow, Pomerania)

### 3.1 Facility Profile & Location
- **Designation:** Kriegsgefangenen-Mannschafts-Stammlager Luft IV (Stalag Luft IV)
- **Administering Authority:** German Air Force (*Luftwaffe*)
- **Location:** Gross Tychow, Province of Pomerania, Prussia (now **Tychowo**, Białogard County, West Pomeranian Voivodeship, Poland: **53°55' N, 16°15' E**).
- **Establishment:** Opened on 12–14 May 1944 to accommodate the overflow of Allied aircrews from Stalag Luft VI (Heydekrug) and Stalag Luft I (Barth).
- **Target Demographics:** Allied Air Force Enlisted Men and Non-Commissioned Officers (NCOs) — USAAF Technical Sergeants, Staff Sergeants, Sergeants (B-17/B-24 radio operators, flight engineers, waist/ball-turret/tail gunners) and RAF/RCAF NCO flight crews.
- **Design Capacity vs. Actual Population:**
  - Planned Capacity: 6,400 POWs.
  - Actual Peak Population (Jan 1945): **Over 10,000 POWs** (7,089 Americans, 866 RAF/Commonwealth, plus Allied medical personnel).

### 3.2 Camp Architecture & Layout
Stalag Luft IV was constructed in a pine forest clearing surrounded by dense Pomeranian woods:

1. **Vorlager (Administration & Outer Camp):**
   - **Kommandantur:** German HQ building, commandant's quarters, and guard administration offices.
   - **Main Gate (*Haupttor*):** Guard post and double-gate security checkpoint.
   - **Garrison Barracks:** Quarters for Luftwaffe guard personnel and SS detachment.
   - **Revier (Infirmary):** 2 wooden huts for sick/wounded Allied airmen staffed by captured Allied medical officers (e.g., Capt. Henry J. Wynsen).
   - **Supply Depots:** Warehouses for German provisions, Red Cross parcel storage, and clothing depots.

2. **POW Compounds (*Lagern A, B, C, D*):**
   - **Lager A, B, C:** Primary American airmen compounds.
   - **Lager D:** Mixed American and RAF/RCAF airmen compound.
   - Each compound was a self-contained rectangle (~250m x 300m) enclosed by internal barbed-wire fencing.
   - Designed for 1,600 POWs per compound, but crowded to 2,300–2,500 men each.

3. **Barracks Huts (*Baracken*):**
   - Approximately **40 single-story wooden barracks huts** across the 4 compounds (~10 huts per compound).
   - Standard dimensions: ~10 meters wide by ~40 meters long.
   - Divided into 10–12 rooms equipped with triple-tiered wooden bunks, straw mattresses, and a small coal stove. Rooms designed for 16 men were jammed with 24 or more men.

4. **Perimeter Security Infrastructure:**
   - **Double Barbed-Wire Fence:** 10-foot-high parallel fences filled with thick coils of German concertina wire.
   - **Neutral Zone / Warning Wire (*Warndraht*):** A low single-strand wire set 5 meters inside the fence line. Any prisoner stepping over the warning wire was shot immediately without warning.
   - **Guard Watchtowers (*Wachtürme*):** 16 timber towers positioned along the perimeter, equipped with high-intensity searchlights, telephone lines, and MG42 machine guns.

### 3.3 Notable Historical Events at Stalag Luft IV

1. **The "Run at Tychow" (July 1944):**
   - On arrival at Gross Tychow railway station, newly arrived POWs were forced by SS-Hauptmann Haupt and Kriegsmarine/Luftwaffe guards to run 2 miles from the station to the camp carrying heavy gear. Guards bayoneted, struck POWs with rifle butts, and set attack dogs on them. Hundreds suffered stab wounds and dog bites.

2. **The "Black March" / Death March (6 Feb – April 1945):**
   - As the Soviet Red Army advanced into Pomerania in early February 1945, the German High Command ordered the evacuation of Stalag Luft IV.
   - Over 8,000 POWs were force-marched on foot in sub-zero winter temperatures across Germany (500 to 800 miles over 86 days). Prisoners suffered severe frostbite, dysentery, starvation, and exhaustion before liberation by British and US forces in April/May 1945.

## 4. Archival Record Correlation: Melvin Samuel "Sam" Hewitt

### 4.1 Primary NARA Record Synthesis
- **Name:** HEWITT, SAM M
- **Serial Number (ASN):** 19179305
- **Grade:** Technical Sergeant (TSgt)
- **Branch:** Army Air Corps (`AC`)
- **Parent Unit:** 301st Bombardment Group (Heavy) — Code `S53 / 301`
- **State of Residence:** California

- **Latest Report Date:** 23 July 1945
- **Status:** Returned to Military Control (Code `8` — Liberated POW)
- **Detaining Power:** Germany
- **POW Camp Location:** Stalag Luft 4 Gross-Tychow Pomerania (moved to Wöbbelin / Usedom) — Code `54-16`

### 4.2 Reconciliation of Family Oral History with Primary Records
- **Family Claim 1: "Radio Operator in the Army"**  
  *Finding:* Verified. TSgt Melvin Samuel "Sam" Hewitt (ASN 19179305) was an Air Corps Technical Sergeant, serving as a B-17 Radio Operator / Gunner.
- **Family Claim 2: "In a Polish concentration camp"**  
  *Finding:* Clarified. The camp was located at Gross Tychow, Pomerania (now **Tychowo, Poland**). While geographically in modern Poland, it was a **Luftwaffe Prisoner of War Camp (Stalag Luft IV)** for Allied airmen, not an SS concentration or extermination camp.
- **Family Claim 3: "Against Poncho / Pancho"**  
  *Finding:* Clarified. Oral tradition garbled the name. This refers either to the **Ploiești** oil fields (Romania), the target of frequent 301st BG raids, or **Pola** drydocks (Italy/Croatia), attacked by 301st BG on 25 June 1944, or **Panchevo/Pančevo** oil facilities on the Danube.
