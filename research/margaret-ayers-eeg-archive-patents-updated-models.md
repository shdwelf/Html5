# Margaret E. Ayers, EEG archives, patents, and updated conceptual models

**Research note · 2 November 2026**

**Scope:** Internet Archive and Project Gutenberg searches; Ayers identity/travel leads; patent-family, claim, and drawing-text review; conceptual updates to the system and research designs.

> **Interpretation of “updated models.”** I treat this as conceptual system/research architecture, not CAD, a circuit schematic, executable software, a clinical protocol, or a build-ready medical device. None of the models below makes a treatment claim or specifies a patient’s EEG targets.

## Executive findings and corrections

- **Paso Robles ranch: yes, it is reported for Margaret E. Ayers.** Her memorial says she kept racehorses and planted a Chardonnay vineyard on a ranch in Paso Robles. I previously over-weighted a separate profile of Paso Robles rancher Margaret Craspay Avila and wrongly treated it as evidence against Ayers having a ranch. It is not. The Ayers memorial is the direct source located; I did not independently corroborate the property, vineyard, or horse through land/title or racing records.
- **Fiji remains unverified.** The documented fishing story I found is in **New Zealand**, not Fiji: the memorial says Ayers caught, tagged, and released a 220-pound blue marlin and received a New Zealand government patch and certificate. That is personal-memorial testimony, not an independently located catch log or travel record. She may have visited Fiji too, but I found no reliable source establishing it.
- **Patent-number correction:** the matching Ayers patent is **US 5,024,235**, not **US 5,024,253**. The latter is a Toyota textile-loom control patent. The one-digit transposition appears in a secondary neurofeedback handout.
- The three distinct U.S. patent publications attributable to the Ayers EEG designs are **US 4,919,143**, **US 5,024,235**, and **US 5,571,057**. The first two have the same title and closely overlapping descriptions, but different applications and claim sets; the first has international counterparts. The middle initial for the second is recorded as “A.” rather than “E.”
- Archive.org is useful for historical engineering and evidence context. Its query for “EEG biofeedback” returned 64 text records, including unrelated material. Project Gutenberg searches for EEG/neurofeedback terms were largely unproductive.

## 1. Paso Robles and the Fiji/New Zealand lead

The [Margaret Ayers memorial](https://celebrantpam.com/memorials/margaretayers.html) identifies Margaret Elaine Ayers and describes her as a Paso Robles rancher, racehorse owner, and vineyard planter. It also recounts the New Zealand marlin trip. Separate local profiles identify **Margaret Craspay Avila**, born in 1932, as another Paso Robles ranching figure ([Paso Robles Press](https://pasoroblespress.com/paso-robles-magazine/93rd-annual-pioneer-day-queen-carries-on-a-tradition-of-community-and-heritage/); [SLO County Farm Bureau](https://www.slofarmbureau.org/business-to-business/article/meet-the-2018-ag-of-the-year-cattleman-of-the-year-and-cattlewoman-of-the-year)). That is a different person, not a reason to erase Ayers’s own reported Paso Robles connection. It remains prudent to attribute the details to the Ayers memorial rather than imply that a deed, ranch name, race record, or vineyard registration has been independently verified.

The same memorial’s travel account is specific: **New Zealand; a 220-pound blue marlin; tagged and released; patch and certificate naming “Maggie Ayers.”** Exact searches for Ayers plus Fiji did not yield a corroborating record. So the answer to “how did the Fiji trip go?” is: **I cannot verify that she took a Fiji trip. If the intended story is the marlin trip, the source places it in New Zealand, and describes it as a successful catch-and-release.** The memorial supplies no year, port, boat, companion, or certificate number, so those details should not be invented.

### Project Gutenberg

This means Gutenberg was unproductive for these exact search terms—not that every potentially relevant historical text is absent from the collection.

### What the archival contrast means

The 1977 critical review and the 1977–78 animal experiments answer different questions. The monkey work supports acquisition of a rewarded EEG pattern in a tightly controlled preclinical setting; the later experiment tests a drug-induced seizure response. Townsend’s review asks whether human EEG changes are direct operant learning and whether changing a band itself improves health. Neither strand, by itself, validates Ayers’s clinical claims. The 1975–76 Navy theses are engineering/task-design records, not clinical trials and not Margaret Ayers’s work.

## 3. Patent inventory and claim/drawing review

“Patent design” is used here in the documentary sense: what the published specifications and claims describe. A patent grant does **not** establish that a device works clinically, that its physiological theory is correct, or that its intended use is safe or effective.

### What is common, and what is not

The 4,919,143 and 5,024,235 descriptions are strikingly close: both outline real-time digitization, frequency selection, threshold/duration logic, raw or processed waveform display, feedback, recording, and artifact suppression. But they should **not** be collapsed into one patent: the filing dates and applications differ, the inventor’s middle initial differs in the record, and the issued claim sets differ (8 apparatus-oriented claims versus 12 method claims). The 4,919,143 family includes the international publication [WO 1990/15571 A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO1990015571A1/en) and national counterparts in Australia, Germany, Great Britain, and Japan; Google Patents’ status labels vary by jurisdiction. The 5,024,235 and 5,571,057 records are separate U.S. applications in the records reviewed.

The written preferred embodiment of the first two patents is historically specific: it names 1980s PC hardware and describes (among other details) a 4–7 Hz trace and a 12–15 Hz trace, with separate inhibit/enhance indicators and reward logic. Those are patent examples—not universal clinical targets. The specification’s assertions that its processing is more accurate or that the invention can diagnose or cure disorders are inventor assertions, not independent evidence.

The 5,571,057 game patent is a distinct interaction design: the player views an image sequence, EEG is measured and thresholded, and the displayed sequence changes when the signal meets the rule. Its 1–500 Hz control/flicker language is not retained in the modern concept below. A modern human-subjects design would need a specialist safety review before using any photic stimulus; there is no reason to reproduce the patent’s flicker mechanism merely to make a game responsive.

### Patent-number and inventor false positives

- **US 5,024,253 A** is [Toyota’s “System for controlling warp take up and let off rates”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5024253A/en), a loom/textile control patent, not EEG or Ayers. It is the likely one-digit error in the secondary handout that lists Ayers’s patents.
- **US 5,406,957 A** is an adjacent neurofeedback patent by **Michael A. Tansey**, not Margaret Ayers. It appears in searches because its citations include the Ayers patents; it should not be added to her portfolio.
- Google Patents marks the three Ayers U.S. records expired. These are database status indications, not legal advice. The 5,571,057 page also records a 2011 court-order assignment to Anne L. Ayers; that entry alone does not explain the underlying order.

## 4. Patent-history dispute: what can and cannot be said

A 2023 *Psychotherapy Networker* account ([“Alice in Neuroland”](https://www.psychotherapynetworker.org/article/alice-neuroland/)) says Ayers sued the Othmers for patent infringement and that the matter was settled through arbitration in 1991. Siegfried Othmer’s own retrospective, [*Milestones in the Development of the Othmer Method*](https://eeg-media.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Milestones+in+the+Development+of+the+Othmer+Method.pdf), also describes a partnership breakdown and a 1991 arbitration, but presents a sharply different account of authorship, alleged copying, and the patent’s validity. The latter is a party’s retrospective, not a court finding; the Networker account is secondary and its full article is access-controlled.

I did not locate a public court docket or the arbitration award that would settle what patents, software, contracts, or claims were actually decided. Therefore: **the dispute and 1991 arbitration are reported by both sources; the disputed allegations and legal outcome remain unresolved in the public material reviewed.** Do not turn Othmer’s “invalid/fake patent” characterization into a statement that the issued Ayers patents were judicially voided.

## 5. Updated conceptual models (research-only, version 0.1)

These updates preserve the historical ideas—live EEG, a feature/threshold rule, and contingent feedback—while adding modern signal-quality controls, provenance, controls, and independently measured outcomes. They intentionally do not prescribe a treatment band or claim therapeutic efficacy.

### Model A — signal-to-feedback architecture

```text
Scalp EEG + reference
(optional EOG / EMG / motion channels for artifact detection)
        │
        ▼
Isolated amplifier + calibrated ADC + timestamps/event synchronization
        │
        ├──────────────► Immutable raw-data buffer + calibration metadata
        ▼
Signal-quality gate
(impedance, saturation, line noise, eye/muscle/motion artifact)
        │
        ├─ invalid/noisy interval ─► mark interval; neutral feedback; no reward
        ▼
Protocol-locked feature estimator
(fully specified band/feature, filter, window, latency, and version)
        │
        ▼
Baseline + documented threshold/reward rule
(no unlogged threshold drift; no automatic interpretation as diagnosis)
        │
        ▼
User-adjustable, non-flashing visual/audio feedback
        │
        ├──────────────► Session/event log + algorithm/configuration version
        └──────────────► Independent outcome measurement, separate from feedback
```

**Updates from the patent-era design:** retain raw data and every threshold/configuration change; use explicit signal-quality states rather than allowing artifacts to trigger rewards; record device, channel, reference, timing, and filter versions; make the 50/60 Hz mains environment configurable; specify the feature and latency before a study; and keep the feedback display non-flashing. A research implementation would require electrical safety, privacy/security, ethics, and performance validation. This diagram is not a hardware bill of materials or a medical-device specification.

### Model B — EEG-contingent image/game interface

```text
EEG + game-event timestamps
        ▼
Quality gate ── invalid signal ──► neutral scene / pause / flagged event
        ▼
Pre-registered EEG feature and threshold rule
        ▼
Game engine changes scene gradually (no flicker-control signal)
        ▼
Separate logs: EEG feature, display transition, task score, adverse events
        ▼
Compare contingent feedback with yoked/sham feedback and an appropriate control
```

This updates US 5,571,057’s image-sequence idea while avoiding its high-frequency flicker control. The game score is not itself evidence that the intended EEG feature was learned; EEG-target change, task performance, expectancy, and any clinical outcome must be analyzed separately. A non-contingent/yoked condition is important because a participant may improve at a game through ordinary practice or expectation.

### Model C — task/performance experiment based on the 1975–76 Naval designs

The old task designs combined a simulated pilot-control task, random disturbances, EEG recording, and a task-error measure. A modern **research** version would preserve that useful experimental separation while making the conditions reproducible:

```text
Seeded perturbation schedule ──► task simulator ──► tracking error / response time
                                              ▲
                                              │ participant controls
EEG + optional eye/muscle channels + synchronized event markers
        ▼
Artifact/QC review and locked feature extraction
        ▼
Randomized/counterbalanced blocks:
  contingent feedback | yoked/sham feedback | task-only baseline
        ▼
Pre-registered analysis of independent task outcomes and EEG changes
```

The test should use a fixed perturbation seed or a prespecified randomization scheme, log task events, counterbalance task difficulty, and treat “preferred frequency” as a hypothesis to test—not a fixed biological fact. It should report signal quality and movement/EMG contamination. This is a research model inspired by Naval Postgraduate School theses; it is not a Margaret Ayers design.

### Model D — evidence/validation ladder

```text
Signal-processing bench validation
        ▼
Human-factors and safety feasibility (ethics review)
        ▼
Pilot study with predefined outcomes and adverse-event monitoring
        ▼
Randomized comparison with sham/yoked feedback and/or standard care
        ▼
Independent replication and condition-specific evidence synthesis
        ▼
Claims limited to the population, protocol, comparator, and outcome actually tested
```

The primary outcome should be clinically or functionally meaningful and measured independently of the feedback display; change in a target EEG band alone is not proof of benefit. Preregister the hypothesis, sample-size rationale, feature/threshold logic, analysis plan, and adverse events; report co-interventions and missing data; analyze by intention to treat where appropriate; and replicate before making broad claims. No patent, animal study, single case, or memorial account substitutes for that chain.

## 6. Bottom line

The documentary record supports three distinct Ayers U.S. patent designs: a real-time thresholded EEG monitor/feedback apparatus; a separate method-oriented patent with a nearly duplicated specification; and a later EEG-contingent visual/game sequence. The patent drawings’ written descriptions and claims are now mapped, with direct patent links for viewing the drawing sheets. Archive.org adds valuable preclinical and engineering context, including a useful contemporary critique, but not proof of broad human treatment effects. Gutenberg supplied no technical EEG records under the terms searched.

The biographical record also changes the earlier conclusion: **Ayers is indeed reported to have had a Paso Robles ranch. The marlin story is reported as New Zealand; a Fiji trip remains unverified.** Updated designs are presented only as conceptual research architectures, not as build-ready or clinically validated systems.
