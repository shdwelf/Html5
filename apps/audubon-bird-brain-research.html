Broken Wing Display & Nest Defense

When a potential predator approaches a ground nest, an adult may call, bob, run away, and perform a broken-wing display. Cornell Lab describes a separate response to hoofed animals: the bird fluffs up, raises and spreads its tail, and runs toward the animal to redirect its path.

BROKEN WING DISPLAY

Parent runs away from nest with one wing drooping and fanned, making distress calls to lure predator away — stops immediately once danger passes

GROUND NESTING

Lays 4 eggs in scraped depressions with minimal nest material, relying entirely on camouflage and behavioral defense

VOCAL DEFENSE

Uses loud piercing "kil-deer" calls to warn mates and disorient predators — adjustable volume and urgency

SPLIT PARENTING

Both parents alternate incubation (24-28 days) and defense roles

CAMOUFLAGE EGGS

Speckled buff-and-brown eggs blend perfectly with gravel, bare ground, and rooftops

COORDINATED STRATEGY

One parent distracts while the other silently incubates or moves chicks

UNGULATE RESPONSE

Fluffs up, displays its tail over its head, and runs toward cattle or horses to redirect their path

CONTEXT-SPECIFIC DEFENSE

The response to a hoofed animal differs from the broken-wing display used against a following predator

🪺 Nesting Pattern

Ground nesting in open areas — gravel, bare earth, parking lots, railroad tracks, or flat rooftops. Minimal nest material; relies entirely on egg camouflage.

⚔️ Defense Style

Broken wing display (distraction), loud vocalizations, direct aerial attack on large animals, coordinated parental defense

👥 Social Behavior

Monogamous pairs work together during breeding; highly vocal and expressive year-round
