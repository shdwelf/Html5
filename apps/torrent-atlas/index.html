PARSE, DON’T LAUNCH

Look inside a magnet.

NO PEER LOOKUP

Inspect v1 hex/base32, SHA-256 v2 and hybrid claims. Copy a minimal identifier with all trackers, web seeds, explicit peers and unknown parameters removed. This does not anonymize a later download.

VERIFY THE METADATA

A file list. Not a leap of faith.

LOCAL MODULE WORKER

Read a local v1, v2 or consistent hybrid torrent. Hashes are computed from the original encoded info bytes. v2 piece layers are checked against their Merkle roots; payload bytes and publisher identity are not verified.

YOUR FILES, YOUR DECISION

Create metadata. Not a swarm.

V1 CREATION

Build a real v1 .torrent from 1–256 explicitly selected files, up to 256 MiB total. Flat multi-file collections are ordered by UTF-8 filename. Piece hashes span file boundaries. Creation never uploads or seeds the files.

Only choose files you intend and have permission to share. Torrent metadata exposes filenames and sizes. Do not add password vaults, private keys or confidential files. A torrent needs a separate seeding client to make the payload available.

OUR BEST-OVERALL PICK · PAID, CROSS-PLATFORM

1Password ↗

Our recommendation for a general Android/browser/desktop user willing to pay: documented dual-key design, broad platform support, sharing and recovery workflows. It is proprietary, hosted, and not immune to malicious-server attacks.

Read the security design ↗

Prefer open source & value?

Bitwarden. Broad cross-device coverage, published audits and a useful free plan. Official self-hosting and the separate Vaultwarden project need operational care.

Want direct local control?

KeePassXC + KeePassDX on Android. Portable encrypted databases. You manage backups, sync conflicts, recovery and the trustworthiness of each client.

Already in Proton?

Proton Pass. Open-source clients and integrated aliases. Check account recovery, exports and passkey portability before migrating.

Important research correction. The full August 2026 revision of IACR 2026/058 reports attacks on all four studied cloud managers, including six for 1Password. “No new attack vectors” is a vendor characterization—not “zero attacks” or proof of immunity. Audit coverage and remediation differ. Read the primary paper ↗

Before putting real credentials in any manager
  1. Install the current signed app/extension from the official vendor or store. Do not get password managers from torrents, mirrors or Internet Archive snapshots.
  2. Choose a unique, long random account passphrase. Keep recovery materials/Secret Key offline in a secure place.
  3. Enable phishing-resistant MFA with two registered hardware keys where supported. Keep an independent recovery route; do not rely on the locked vault to unlock itself.
  4. Trial autofill on your actual devices with dummy logins; match full domains. Check exports, attachments and passkey transfer limitations.
  5. Test an encrypted backup and restore before deleting an old vault. CSV and many migration exports are plaintext—handle locally and carefully; deletion is not guaranteed secure erasure.
  6. Keep high-value account recovery and independent second factors separate where your threat model requires it. No manager protects an already-compromised unlocked endpoint.
