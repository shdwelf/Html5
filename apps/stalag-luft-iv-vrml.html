Stalag Luft IV Overview
Gross Tychow, Pomerania (now Tychowo, Poland)

Luftwaffe prisoner of war camp constructed in May 1944 to hold captured Allied aircrews. Divided into 4 main compounds (Lagern A, B, C, D) housing over 8,000 American and 800 British airmen.

ORAL TRADITION VS PRIMARY ARCHIVAL RECORD
CLAIM 1 "In a Polish Concentration Camp"

Historical Fact: Stalag Luft IV was located in Gross Tychow, Pomerania — territory that became part of Tychowo, Poland after WWII borders were redrawn. It was a Luftwaffe Prisoner of War Camp for Allied airmen, not an SS concentration or extermination camp.

CLAIM 2 "Maurice Was a Radio Operator Against Pancho"

Historical Fact: Maurice Hewitt (ASN 19067302) enlisted as a carpenter. It was his brother Melvin Samuel "Sam" Hewitt (ASN 19179305) who served as the heavy bomber Radio Operator / Gunner. "Pancho" phonetically garbles the Ploiești oil refineries (Romania) or Pola drydocks.

VERIFIED Adjacent Enlistment & Joint Service

Both brothers enlisted in California in the Army Air Corps (ASNs 19-067-302 and 19-179-305). Sam was shot down on 26 June 1944 over Austria and survived 10 months of POW captivity including the "Black March".

STALAG LUFT IV HISTORICAL TIMELINE & NOTABLE EVENTS
1. "The Run at Tychow" (July 1944)

Upon arrival at Gross Tychow railway station, newly arriving Allied airmen POWs were forced by SS and Kriegsmarine guards to run 2 miles to camp with heavy baggage under bayonets, dog bites, and rifle butt blows. Over 600 prisoners sustained injuries.

2. "The Black March" / Death March (Feb–April 1945)

As Soviet forces advanced across Pomerania, 8,000 Allied airmen were force-marched on foot in sub-zero winter temperatures across Germany for 86 days over 500+ miles. Starvation and dysentery were rampant until liberation by British/US forces.
