    CIA GROUNDS — FULL INSTALLATION

    Kryptos is part of a larger Sanborn installation across the CIA's New Headquarters Building courtyard and entrance plaza. The full installation includes:

    • Main sculpture (courtyard NW corner): copper screen, red granite, water basin, petrified wood, lodestone
    • Morse code slabs (entrance walkway): copper sheets between granite, International Morse code: "SOS", "LUCID MEMORY", "T IS YOUR POSITION", "SHADOW FORCES", "VIRTUALLY INVISIBLE", "DIGETAL INTERPRETATU" (deliberate misspelling), "RQ"/"YR"
    • Compass rose (entrance): carved in granite, needle points to a buried lodestone (magnetic north reference)
    • Fish pond with opposing wooden benches
    • Landscaped garden ("peaceful contemplative hidden space" — Sanborn)
    • Triangular black stone slab in the courtyard
    • Berlin Wall slabs (dedicated 1992): three segments gifted by German government
    • Coordinates in K2 (38°57'06.5"N, 77°08'44"W) point ~150 ft southeast of sculpture

    K5 — THE RIDDLE WITHIN THE RIDDLE

    Sanborn has confirmed K5 exists — a fifth coded message that will be revealed after K4 is solved. In an open letter (August 2025), Sanborn stated:

    "K5 will have a more global reach... will be publicly accessible... and will use a similar cryptographic system to K4. The words BERLINCLOCK will be in the same position."

    "Two events had a significant role to play while I was writing the plain text of Kryptos in 1988. The first was my second trip to Egypt in late 1986, and the second was the fall of the Berlin Wall."

    "The codes of Kryptos... are about delivering a message."
    — Jim Sanborn, Open Letter, August 2025

    K5 context: K2 plaintext says "its buried out there somewhere" — possibly referring to K5 being physically hidden on CIA grounds.

    2025–2026 K4 BREAKTHROUGH

    How K4 Was "Solved"

    In September 2025, writers Jarett Kobek and Richard Byrne visited the Smithsonian's Archives of American Art to photograph Sanborn's donated papers (tipped off by the auction listing). They found scraps of paper with K4 plaintext — Sanborn had accidentally included his notes while hastily assembling archives during cancer treatment. They emailed Sanborn the solution on Sept 3, 2025. Sanborn confirmed it was correct.

    Key facts: The method remains secret. The winning auction bidder ($962,500) gets the method. Smithsonian sealed the archives for 50 years (until ~2075). Kobek & Byrne pledged not to publish.

    As Elonka Dunin said: "If they don't have the method, it's not solved."
