# Research: Geneva Conventions, the UN, the International Court of Justice, and the "South African Court of Justice"

*Research date: 2026-10-06 · Companion to [`territorial-classification-deep-dive-2026-10-06.md`](./territorial-classification-deep-dive-2026-10-06.md) · Viewer: [`jurisdictions-viewer.html`](../jurisdictions-viewer.html)*

## 0. Disambiguation first (source-check culture)

The prompt chains four names that are routinely conflated; this file keeps them strictly separate:

1. **Geneva Conventions (1949)** — four treaties on international humanitarian law (wounded/sick on land; wounded/sick/shipwrecked at sea; prisoners of war; **GC IV: civilians**), plus Additional Protocols I–II (1977) and III (2005). Universally ratified (196 parties). Enforced primarily through national courts (grave-breaches regime), the ICRC's protective mandate, and — for individuals — the ICC. **The Geneva Conventions are not what *South Africa v. Israel* is filed under.**
2. **Genocide Convention (1948)** — the Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide; its **Article IX** sends state-vs-state disputes to the ICJ. *This* is the treaty basis of the South Africa case. (Adopted Dec. 9, 1948 — one day before the Universal Declaration; both predate the 1949 Geneva Conventions.)
3. **International Court of Justice (ICJ)** — the UN's principal judicial organ (UN Charter ch. XIV; seat: Peace Palace, The Hague; 15 judges; president since 2024–25 term: Yuji Iwasawa). Hears **states only** (contentious cases) + advisory opinions for UN organs. Distinct from the **ICC** (individual criminal liability, Rome Statute 1998).
4. **"South African Court of Justice"** — no court bears this name. The phrase resolves three ways, all covered in § 3: (a) **South Africa *at* the International Court of Justice** (the genocide case — almost certainly the intended sense); (b) South Africa's own apex courts (**Constitutional Court**; Supreme Court of Appeal); (c) the **African Court of Justice** — an African Union court that was merged on paper into the African Court of Justice and Human Rights (Malabo Protocol lineage) but **has never come into existence**; the operating AU court is the African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights (Arusha).

## 1. *South Africa v. Israel* (ICJ) — status as of 2026-10-06

**Application of the Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide in the Gaza Strip (South Africa v. Israel)**, filed **December 29, 2023**, invoking Article IX and standing *erga omnes partes* (any state party may enforce; the ICJ accepted that logic in *The Gambia v. Myanmar*, 2020–22).

| Outlook | Further written rounds (reply/rejoinder) reported to run into **2027–2029**; merits hearings and judgment after that. The late-2025 Gaza ceasefire did **not** end the case (South Africa confirmed continuation). |

**Legal architecture to keep straight:** provisional measures are binding (*LaGrand*, 2001) but are expressly *not* a finding that genocide occurred; enforcement runs through the UN Security Council (Charter art. 94(2)), where a veto is available; the court's "plausibility" standard from the Jan. 2024 order is about the rights asserted, not the merits.

### 1.1 The adjacent ICJ matters (often mixed into the same conversation)

- **Advisory opinion, July 19, 2024** — *Legal Consequences arising from the Policies and Practices of Israel in the Occupied Palestinian Territory*: occupation unlawful; obligation to end it "as rapidly as possible." Advisory = non-binding, but feeds UNGA resolutions.
- **Advisory opinion on Israel's obligations re UN presence/humanitarian assistance** (UNGA request Dec. 2024; proceedings 2025 — the matter Israel cited in its extension request; delivered Oct. 2025). *Grade C: verify delivery date and holdings at icj-cij.org before formal citation.*
- ***The Gambia v. Myanmar*** — the *erga omnes partes* precedent making South Africa's standing uncontroversial as a threshold matter.
- **Geneva-Conventions touchpoint:** the 2024 advisory opinion and the provisional-measures orders both invoke IHL (GC IV) *alongside* the Genocide Convention — the two regimes meet in the facts while remaining separate in jurisdiction: the ICJ's Article IX jurisdiction here reaches only genocide claims, not IHL violations as such.

## 2. The UN layer

- ICJ = principal judicial organ (Charter arts. 7, 92–96); all UN members are *ipso facto* parties to the ICJ Statute.
- **Security Council**: enforcement of judgments (art. 94(2)); U.S. vetoes have blocked Gaza-related measures — the structural gap between adjudication and enforcement is the recurring theme of every entry in this research package.
- **General Assembly**: requests advisory opinions (art. 96), channels the 2024 AO into resolutions; CARICOM (cross-link to the companion file) holds UNGA observer status as a bloc.
- The **ICRC** — guardian of the Geneva Conventions — operates entirely outside this judicial machinery; the Conventions' own dispute mechanisms (common art. 1 "respect and ensure respect," the never-used International Humanitarian Fact-Finding Commission of AP I art. 90) underline why genocide litigation goes to the ICJ while IHL enforcement mostly doesn't.

## 3. The "South African Court of Justice," all three readings

**(a) South Africa at the ICJ** — § 1 above; the state's most consequential international litigation since the end of apartheid, prosecuted by a country whose own constitutional order (below) was built on international-law incorporation (Const. ss. 231–233).

**(b) South Africa's domestic courts** — apex: the **Constitutional Court** (Braamfontein, 11 justices; since 2013 the final court in *all* matters, not just constitutional); below it the **Supreme Court of Appeal** (Bloemfontein) and High Court divisions. Two rulings matter directly to this file's theme of states being bound by the classifications they join:
- *Minister of Justice v. Southern African Litigation Centre* (SCA, 2016): the government acted unlawfully in failing to arrest President al-Bashir (ICC warrant) during the 2015 AU summit — domestic courts enforcing Rome Statute obligations against their own executive.
- *Democratic Alliance v. Minister of International Relations* (Gauteng HC, 2017): the executive's notice of **withdrawal from the ICC** without parliamentary approval was unconstitutional; the withdrawal was revoked. (South Africa remains an ICC party as of this research date.)

**(c) The African Court of Justice** — the AU's 2003 protocol created a Court of Justice that never operated; the 2008 merger protocol (African Court of Justice and Human Rights) and the 2014 **Malabo Protocol** (adding a criminal chamber with immunity for sitting heads of state — the controversial art. 46A bis) have **not reached the ratification threshold**. The functioning continental court is the **African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights** (Arusha, est. 2004 operational 2006), with jurisdiction accepted by a shrinking set of states. Any citation to a "South African Court of Justice" in draft text should be corrected to whichever of (a)–(c) is meant.
