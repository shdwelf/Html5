## Why an induction, and why now
The hall's recurring lesson is that puzzles fall to provenance work as
often as to mathematics. Cicada is the limiting case: an unattributed,
still-unfinished puzzle whose *record* — not solution — is the durable
artefact. The 2012 poster layer is the sharpest example: fourteen
coordinates published at 17:00 UTC on 9 January 2012, paper posters on
four continents, five of them photographed and ledgered as FOUND by the
solver community (Warsaw, Paris, Fayetteville, Miami, Seoul–Gangseo),
two coordinates carried only by the website, and every physical artefact
since removed or weathered away. What can still be checked — coordinates,
timestamps, photographs, archived pages — is exactly what the record
recites, and its caution keeps the solver-attested status of the outcomes
in view.

## Boundaries (identical to the Cicada pack's)
No QR payloads, no onion addresses, no puzzle solution material; the
Liber Primus is named only to say it remains unsolved and untouched. The
onion-service stages are recorded as unrecoverable by design — the same
refusal the Warrick planner encodes.
