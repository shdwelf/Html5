# Spectre Press, kimsoft.com — domain-chain source check

**Run:** 28 September 2026. **Scope:** the spectrepress.com research thread,
written up as a domain-chain source check in the house style (named entity →
what the primary captures actually establish → verdict). **Provenance
note:** the research conclusions from the session that opened this thread
did not survive into this one, so every fact below was re-verified fresh
against Wayback captures and current pages; nothing is carried forward on
trust. All URLs cited as the capture actually used.

## 1. Spectre Press — verdict in one line

**spectrepress.com was a real small-press RPG publisher in 2001–2003**
("fun and affordable games and accessories", copyright line 2001), whose
catalog and at least one product-line name survive today inside **Precis
Intermedia (pigames.net)**, which now operates the spectrepress.com domain
as a pointer to itself. It is *not* a hacker-zine or security imprint —
despite the name, there is no connection to the protection/packer corpus
this repo archives; any such association is a false lead.

## 2. What the captures establish

### 2.1 The publisher, 2002

Wayback: [2002-06-03 capture of http://www.spectrepress.com/](https://web.archive.org/web/20020603180041/http://www.spectrepress.com/)
(128 captures of the domain overall, 03 Jun 2002 – 17 Sep 2026).

- Self-description: *"Spectre Press delivers fun and affordable games and
  accessories for a wide variety of gaming genres."*
- Announced **Eric Petracca as Lead Editor** ("skilled writer and editor…
  hard-working").
- Site sections: Home / Tools / Games / Contact — a four-page site.

### 2.2 The catalog

**Gamer's Survival Kit** ([tools.htm, 2002-06-07](https://web.archive.org/web/20020607074905/http://www.spectrepress.com/tools.htm)):
a PDF line of "odds and ends you can use to add life to your game",
zipped for download, with a free sample Kit and **Survival Kit #1 at
$5.00 US**. Kit #1 contents, verbatim from the capture:

| component | item |
| --- | --- |
| NPC | Lance P. Bellwether III — "a criminal mastermind for your futuristic game" |
| Silver Bullet | The Thogrot — "a brutal alien creature for nearly any genre" |
| Rogues Gallery | Space Rogues — ten illustrations |
| Thrilling Tales | U.N.Forgiven — "a futuristic supers adventure using the Phantom conversion rules" |
| paper miniatures | **Disposable Heroes** — thirty standup miniatures |
| fiction | The Chatterlings — "a tale of science fiction, complete with Phantom statistics" |

**The Realm of Arkular** ([games.htm, 2002-06-07](https://web.archive.org/web/20020607074738/http://www.spectrepress.com/games.htm)):
"a fantasy setting with the emphasis on the maps," sold as a customizable
PDF (continent → region → city maps), teased with a free screensaver of a
dragon soaring over Arkular. **Phantom Conversion Rules**: a free
universal-conversion ruleset the press promised to use across all its
generic supplements — "prepare to add spirit to your games."

### 2.3 The chain to today

- **RPGGeek** ([publisher #21702](https://rpggeek.com/rpgpublisher/21702/spectre-press)):
  *"A publisher of generic fantasy campaign materials, now a part of
  Precis Intermedia."*
- **spectrepress.com today** ([2026-09-17 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20260917035715/https://www.spectrepress.com/)):
  a single page — "THE OFFICIAL HOME OF" a logo linking through
  rpg.deals/drivethrusp, **OR VISIT pigames.net**, plus a contact link.
  The publisher's page is gone; the domain is a pointer.
- **pigames.net today** resolves as *"Welcome to Precis Intermedia"* and —
  on its own bot-check page — offers contact *"via Facebook or
  **Spectre Press**"* (spectrepress.com). The two domains are operated
  together, which is as direct a confirmation of the absorption as the
  live web still gives.
- **Continuity detail:** "Disposable Heroes," the paper-miniature line
  inside Spectre Press's 2002 Survival Kit #1, is the name Precis
  Intermedia's catalog still carries for its paper-miniatures products —
  the clearest artifact-level line from the 2001 press to the present
  publisher.

## 3. kimsoft.com — verdict in one line

**kimsoft.com is the long-lived "Korea WebWeekly" / kim-spy site** — a
Korean-politics and intelligence link resource published since 1996 by the
Korean Nationalists Association — still resolving in 2026. It has **no
verified connection to Spectre Press**.

What the captures establish ([1998-12-07](https://web.archive.org/web/19981207023156/http://kimsoft.com/korea.htm),
[1999-02-24](https://web.archive.org/web/19990224142042/http://www.kimsoft.com/korea.htm);
679 captures 1997–2026):

- Masthead: *"Korea WebWeekly — An independent, non-partisan, FREE web on
  all things Korean: Her history, culture, economy, politics and military
  — since 1996. Published by the Korean Nationalists Association."*
- Content mix in the 1998–99 captures: Cheju April 3rd massacre / Gen. Kim
  Ik Ruhl documents, US-germ-warfare claims, DPRK pages, comfort-women and
  KCTU labor news, and dense link farms to Korean and intelligence
  resources.
- The **kim-spy** section ("Intelligence and CounterIntelligence") is the
  site's best-known artifact and still resolves today
  (kimsoft.com/kim-spy/), linking declassified handbooks, surveillance and
  countersurveillance material — which is presumably how the name entered
  this repo's intelligence-lore orbit.

## 5. Why this check was run this way

This repo's archive discipline (`samples/archive/PROVENANCE.md`) fetches
old vendor bytes with the Wayback `id_` modifier and refuses to trust a
capture beyond what its digest proves. A domain-chain source check is the
same discipline for *publishers*: spectrepress.com's 2002 pages prove a
small RPG press; its 2026 pointer page and pigames.net's reciprocal link
prove who operates the name now; RPGGeek supplies the secondary
cataloguing; and everything else — including any hacker-culture
association the name might suggest — is marked as unsupported until a
capture says otherwise.
