## Identity and provenance

`xxx.lanl.gov` was the original Los Alamos address of the e-print archive that continues as arXiv. The historical address should therefore be treated as a provenance clue and translated to stable arXiv identifiers when possible. The current cryptography stream is **cs.CR — Cryptography and Security**.

An arXiv deposit is a versioned preprint record, not proof of peer review. The Lecture Hall now explicitly separates:

1. deposited manuscript/version evidence;
2. conference or journal publication evidence; and
3. teaching notes, which may intentionally simplify or omit details.

## Recommended prerequisite ladder

University reading lists from Columbia, NYU and CMU converge on these prerequisites:

1. reading and writing mathematical proofs;
2. discrete mathematics and modular arithmetic;
3. basic probability and counting;
4. algorithms and asymptotic notation;
5. introductory computational complexity;
6. programming for applied labs, although theory-only courses may omit it.

### Core shelf

- **Katz and Lindell, _Introduction to Modern Cryptography_** — bookstore/library-reserve introductory text in Columbia's course.
- **Boneh and Shoup, _A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography_** — authorized open graduate reader with appendices covering number theory, probability, complexity and probabilistic algorithms.
- **Menezes, van Oorschot and Vanstone, _Handbook of Applied Cryptography_** — authorized free chapters; valuable as a broad reference, but older algorithms and recommendations must be checked against current standards.
- **Pass–Shelat, Rosulek, Smart, Trevisan, Goldwasser–Bellare and Bellare–Rogaway** — open readers/notes listed by university courses.
- **Goldreich, _Foundations of Cryptography_** — advanced theory reference after the introductory layer.

## Seminar and convention readers promoted into the hall

- **arXiv:2304.03541**, Thomas Debris-Alazard, _Code-based Cryptography: Lecture Notes_ — ENS Lyon and Budapest post-quantum summer-school material centered on decoding hardness.
- **arXiv:1711.04062**, Luca De Feo, _Mathematics of Isogeny Based Cryptography_ — Thiès summer-school guide for master's students, explicitly intended to be supplemented by books.

These demonstrate a useful reading order: prerequisites → general cryptography text → specialist school reader → current papers and cryptanalysis.
