## 3. Scouting leads and limits

The public resume lists **“BSA Of America Cub Scout”** and **“Order Of The Arrow”** as skills. Those are self-reported resume entries; they do not name a pack, troop, council, lodge/chapter, dates, rank, award, or membership record.

The San Gabriel Valley Council (#40) is a strong **geographic lead** for Glendora-era BSA research. Its local history runs from 1951 to its 2015 merger into Greater Los Angeles Area Council. The former council's Order of the Arrow lodge was **Ta Tanka Lodge #488**; the current Tuku’ut Lodge history says Ta Tanka was chartered in 1952 and grew out of the council's earlier Tribe of Torqua at Camp Cherry Valley. Glendora Troop 483's own history describes its long service in the San Gabriel Valley Council and several districts, including Valle Del Sol. A 2012 Ta Tanka lodge packet associates the Teton Dakota Chapter with Valle Del Sol/Trails of the Valley.

Taken together, these sources make **San Gabriel Valley Council / Ta Tanka #488 / Teton Dakota** a sensible route to check *if* the resume's Scouting entries refer to Glendora units in that period. They do not prove that the user belonged to Troop 483, that this was their chapter, or that they were inducted into the OA. The public name search through SearXNG mirrors surfaced the resume, but no independent personal Scouting record.

Girl Scouts are a separate organization. The Girl Scouts of Greater Los Angeles Heritage Committee says its holdings include predecessor-council history; its 2008 predecessors included **Spanish Trails** and **Mt. Wilson Vista**. A Glendora-area trails reference identifies the Mary Lou Salyer Nature Center with Spanish Trails Girl Scout Council, making Spanish Trails a local archival lead. This still does not identify a personal troop or establish that the user was a Girl Scout; the resume's Scouting self-description is BSA/OA, not Girl Scouts.

For personal records, the most useful next evidence would be a remembered pack/troop number, den/troop leader, patch/card, yearbook photo, or family-held paper. A request to the current GLAAC council or Tuku’ut lodge historian can be made by the user directly; no sensitive identifier is needed in this chat.

## 4. Scout-book candidates, access, and Drive

Catalog searches found plausible but unconfirmed candidates:

- *The Boy Scout Handbook*, 10th edition (Robert C. Birkby, 1990), catalogued by Internet Archive as a **print-disabled/access-restricted** book.
- *Cadette Girl Scout Handbook* (Toni Eubanks / GSUSA, 1995), catalogued in Internet Archive's borrow collection.
- *Girl Scout Badges and Signs* (GSUSA, 1990), also catalogued in a controlled/print-disabled collection.

These records let us identify editions, not treat the scans as unrestricted download files. No book was downloaded, copied to Drive, or placed in the repository. The earlier Drive searches found no filename containing “scout” or “American Land”; the six broad `campfire` full-text hits were not verified as the requested handbook and did not identify one.

`libresearch.space` presents a SearXNG search page. I tested it alongside BareSearch, INETOL SearXNG, and searxng.site for the public name and Wrightwood phrase. BareSearch returned the public resume as the exact-name hit; INETOL and searxng.site returned unrelated “Steven” results after dropping or mishandling the exact-name query. The mirrors did not supply independent evidence or an authorized book copy. They are useful only as search front ends; Internet Archive/library access terms still apply.

A legal next route is a borrow/preview through a library or Internet Archive, a user-provided copy the user is entitled to use, or an explicitly public-domain/openly licensed edition. I will not copy a controlled-lending or pirated scan into Drive. If the user owns a physical book, selected photos/pages or a title/ISBN can be supplied for a personal, locally used viewer; public distribution of scans or copyrighted page art would need separate rights.

## 6. “Magnolia–La Cumbre Lodge” is Masonic, not OA

The resume places **“Santa Barbara La Cumbre Magnolia Lodge No. 192”** in its Masonic volunteer/visitor register alongside a County Fair Child ID booth. The official Santa Barbara lodge history says Magnolia Lodge #242 and La Cumbre later combined as **Magnolia–La Cumbre Lodge #242** (by 1990); on **7 January 2006**, that lodge consolidated with Santa Barbara Lodge #192 and retained Santa Barbara's name and number. The resume's wording appears to blend the intermediate combined name with the later #192 name. It describes a lodge-related activity, not proof of membership.

This Masonic history is unrelated to the BSA Order of the Arrow lodge: Ta Tanka #488 was an OA lodge, while Magnolia–La Cumbre/Santa Barbara #192 was Masonic. Public sources disagree on La Cumbre's separate predecessor lodge number (#142 versus #642); the combined #242 and 2006 consolidation are supported by the Santa Barbara lodge's own history, so the separate number should be left unresolved unless the lodge or Grand Lodge record confirms it.
