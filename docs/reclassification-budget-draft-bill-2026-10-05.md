# Model Congressional Spending Budget & Draft Legislation: Reclassification Implementation

*Research date: 2026-10-05 · Companion to [`federal-reclassification-deep-dive-2026-10-05.md`](./federal-reclassification-deep-dive-2026-10-05.md)*

> **Honesty banner.** This file is a **drafting and budgeting exercise** in the style of a committee print. Structural facts (which accounts exist, what they fund, how riders work) are sourced; every dollar figure is **[ILLUSTRATIVE]** — bracketed the way real draft appropriations text brackets unsettled numbers — unless a source is cited. Nothing here is advocacy; the draft bill includes provisions each side would demand, so it would satisfy neither.

## 1. Where reclassification money actually lives

| Account | Funding type | Relevance to the reclassification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEA **Diversion Control Fee Account** (DCFA) | Fee-funded (registrant fees, 21 U.S.C. § 886a) — *not* taxpayer appropriations | The April 2026 order's expedited registration of state-medical licensees runs through this account; each new Schedule III registrant pays fees into it |
| DEA Salaries & Expenses (CJS appropriations) | Discretionary | Enforcement posture against remaining Schedule I (adult-use/unlicensed) marijuana |
| FDA Salaries & Expenses + PDUFA user fees | Discretionary + user fees | Any future cannabis drug approvals; eight-factor evaluations |
| NIH / NIDA | Discretionary | Research expansion contemplated by EO 14370 and Pub. L. 117-215 |
| GAO | Legislative-branch appropriation | The Sept. 23, 2026 scheduling-process report and recommended follow-ups |
| Treasury/IRS | Discretionary | § 280E transition guidance, amended-return processing for medical operators |
| **Revenue side** (JCT scoring, not an account) | — | Removing § 280E exposure for Schedule III activity is a **revenue decrease**; re-imposing it (the "No Deductions" bill) is a **revenue increase** |

### 1.2 The riders (the enacted congressional "budget positions")

1. **Medical-marijuana rider** (Rohrabacher–Blumenauer lineage, first enacted FY2015): no CJS funds may be used to prevent states from implementing medical-marijuana laws — judicially enforced in *United States v. McIntosh*, 833 F.3d 1163 (9th Cir. 2016).
2. **Harris rider**: blocks D.C. from commercializing adult-use.
3. **FY2026 hemp THC redefinition** (Nov. 2025): the opposite-direction rider — Congress *tightening* a cannabinoid definition while the executive loosens marijuana's schedule.

These three riders are the entire enacted congressional fiscal position on cannabis classification — a limitation-of-funds position, not an authorization position. The draft bill below is what an *authorization* position would look like.

## 2. Draft legislation (model bill — drafting exercise)

```
                    [MODEL BILL — DRAFTING EXERCISE — NOT INTRODUCED]

                               119TH CONGRESS, 2D SESSION
                                        S. ____

  To provide for congressional oversight of controlled-substance reclassification,
  to conform penal provisions of the Controlled Substances Act to schedule
  transfers, and for other purposes.

  SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.
    This Act may be cited as the "Controlled Substances Reclassification
    Transparency and Conforming Amendments Act of 2026".

  SEC. 2. FINDINGS.
    Congress finds the following:
      (1) On May 21, 2024, the Department of Justice published a notice of
          proposed rulemaking (89 Fed. Reg. 44597) to transfer marijuana from
          schedule I to schedule III of the Controlled Substances Act.
      (2) On December 18, 2025, the President issued Executive Order 14370.
      (3) On April 23, 2026, the Attorney General, acting under section
          201(d)(1) of the Controlled Substances Act (21 U.S.C. 811(d)(1)),
          issued a final order placing certain marijuana in schedule III.
      (4) The Comptroller General reported on September 23, 2026, that neither
          the Drug Enforcement Administration nor the Food and Drug
          Administration maintains comprehensive written scheduling policies.
      (5) The penalty structure of section 401(b) of the Controlled Substances
          Act (21 U.S.C. 841(b)) refers to marihuana by name and quantity and
          is not conformed automatically by a transfer between schedules.

  SEC. 3. CONFORMING AMENDMENTS TO PENALTY PROVISIONS.
    (a) Section 401(b)(1) of the Controlled Substances Act (21 U.S.C.
        841(b)(1)) is amended by inserting at the end the following:
        "(F) RULE OF CONSTRUCTION.—In the case of a substance transferred
        between schedules after the date of enactment of this subparagraph,
        the penalty applicable to an offense involving that substance shall be
        the penalty prescribed for the schedule in which the substance was
        placed at the time of the offense, or the substance-specific penalty,
        whichever the court determines Congress most recently prescribed;
        and the Commission shall conform the sentencing guidelines
        accordingly within 180 days.".
    (b) GUN-POSSESSION CROSS-REFERENCE STUDY.—Not later than 1 year after
        enactment, the Attorney General shall report on the interaction of
        section 922(g)(3) of title 18 with substances transferred to
        schedule III, IV, or V.

  SEC. 4. CONGRESSIONAL LAYOVER FOR SCHEDULING ORDERS.
    Section 201(d) of the Controlled Substances Act (21 U.S.C. 811(d)) is
    amended by adding at the end:
      "(6) An order under paragraph (1) placing a substance in a schedule
      lower than the schedule recommended by the most recent evaluation under
      subsection (b) shall not take effect until 60 calendar days after
      transmittal to the Committees on the Judiciary of the Senate and the
      House of Representatives.".

  SEC. 5. WRITTEN SCHEDULING POLICIES.
    Not later than 180 days after enactment, the Administrator of the Drug
    Enforcement Administration and the Commissioner of Food and Drugs shall
    each publish the comprehensive written scheduling policies recommended by
    the Comptroller General in the report of September 23, 2026.

  SEC. 6. SAVINGS CLAUSE.
    Nothing in this Act shall be construed to legalize any substance, to
    affect any State law, or to ratify or disapprove the order of April 23,
    2026.

  SEC. 7. AUTHORIZATION OF APPROPRIATIONS.
    There are authorized to be appropriated to carry out this Act
    $[183,500,000] for fiscal year 2027, to remain available until expended.
```

**Drafting notes.** § 3(a) addresses the (D)-vs-(E) penalty anomaly identified in the deep dive (§ 3.3) — the one place where the penal code genuinely needs *recreating* after reclassification; its "whichever … most recently prescribed" formulation is deliberately the kind of compromise language that would draw fire from both sides. § 4 is the opposition's layover demand; § 5 is the GAO report enacted; § 6 is why neither coalition would ever cosponsor it as written.
