### Jurisdictional observations

1. **The criminal docket is the quiet one.** Every *live* fight is civil or administrative; the criminal layer (C1–C3) is precedent being invoked, not prosecuted. Rider D2 + *McIntosh* (C3) largely suspended federal medical-marijuana prosecutions years before any rescheduling.
2. **Federalism is running in both directions at once.** States appear as *supporters* (F1, F2, F4 — the predicate regimes), and as *opponents* (F3 — AGs suing to undo the federal order that deferred to other states' regimes).
3. **The D.C. Circuit is the chokepoint.** CSA § 877 channels review there; the same court that decided *NORML* (1977) and *ASA* (2013) will decide whether the § 811(d)(1) pathway sustains the April order.
4. **Congress has never voted on the question directly.** Its only enacted positions are appropriations riders (D2, D3) and adjacent statutes (D4, D5, D6) — classification keeps being decided in the executive and the courts precisely because the recodification lesson of § 1 of the deep dive (Congress struggles to pass comprehensive penal reclassification) still holds.
