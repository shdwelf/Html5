## 1. The Organic Acts: how the U.S. classifies its territories

An **organic act** is the congressional statute that constitutes ("organizes") a government for a territory — the territorial analogue of a constitution, granted rather than adopted. Congress acts under the Territorial Clause (U.S. Const. art. IV, § 3, cl. 2). The ladder, as applied to Puerto Rico:

| Instrument | Date | What it (re)classified |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treaty of Paris, art. IX | Dec. 10, 1898 | Spain cedes Puerto Rico; Congress to determine "the civil rights and political status of the native inhabitants" |
| **Foraker Act (Organic Act of 1900)** | Apr. 12, 1900 | First civil government: presidentially appointed governor and Executive Council, elected House of Delegates, Resident Commissioner in Washington. No U.S. citizenship. |
| *Downes v. Bidwell*, 182 U.S. 244 | 1901 | The Insular Cases invent the **"unincorporated territory"** class: Puerto Rico "belongs to, but is not a part of" the United States; the Constitution does not fully follow the flag |
| **Jones–Shafroth Act (Organic Act of 1917)** | Mar. 2, 1917 | U.S. citizenship (collective naturalization), a bill of rights, elected bicameral legislature |
| *Balzac v. Porto Rico*, 258 U.S. 298 | 1922 | Citizenship notwithstanding, Puerto Rico remains unincorporated (no jury-trial right) |
| **Public Law 81-600** | 1950 | "In the nature of a compact": authorizes a Puerto Rican constitution; the surviving organic-act residue is renamed the **Puerto Rican Federal Relations Act** |
| Constitution of the Commonwealth (Estado Libre Asociado) | Jul. 25, 1952 | Internal self-government; the external classification — unincorporated territory — is unchanged, as the modern Supreme Court keeps confirming (*Sánchez Valle* 2016, *Aurelius* 2020, *Vaello Madero* 2022) |

The same device organized the District of Columbia (Organic Acts of 1801 and 1871), Hawaii (1900), Alaska (1912), the Philippines (1902 → Jones Act 1916 → Tydings–McDuffie 1934 → independence 1946), **Guam (Organic Act of Aug. 1, 1950)** and the **U.S. Virgin Islands (Revised Organic Act of 1954)**. The control case is **American Samoa — no organic act at all**: formally "unorganized," governed under a locally adopted 1967 constitution with Interior Department oversight. The recodification lesson of the 2026-10-05 deep dive recurs exactly: **the classification layer (incorporated/unincorporated, organized/unorganized) is separate from the self-government text layer**, and the classification layer is the one the classified polity cannot amend.

## 2. Bermuda as a Virginia colony

Bermuda enters English law **as part of Virginia**:

1. **July 1609** — the *Sea Venture*, flagship of the Virginia Company's Third Supply to Jamestown, wrecks on Bermuda's reefs in a hurricane (Admiral Sir George Somers, Sir Thomas Gates aboard; the episode is a probable source for *The Tempest*). The castaways build the *Deliverance* and *Patience* and reach Jamestown in 1610.
2. **March 12, 1612** — the Virginia Company's **Third Charter** extends Virginia's boundaries to islands within ~300 leagues of its coast, expressly sweeping in Bermuda. The islands are briefly styled **"Virgineola"**, then the **Somers Isles**. The *Plough* delivers the first deliberate settlers under Governor Richard Moore to St. George's in 1612.
3. **1615** — Bermuda is spun off from Virginia to a daughter corporation, the **Somers Isles Company** ("the Governour and Company of the City of London for the Plantacion of the Somers Isles") — a corporate *reclassification*, Virginia dependency → separate proprietary colony.
4. **August 1, 1620** — the Bermudian **House of Assembly** first sits at St. Peter's Church, St. George's: the oldest continuously operating legislature in the Americas, and after Westminster and Tynwald among the oldest in the world.
5. **1684** — the Somers Isles Company's charter is annulled; Bermuda becomes a **Crown colony**. St. George's (UNESCO World Heritage, 2000) remains the oldest continuously inhabited English town in the New World.
6. **Today** — a **British Overseas Territory** (the oldest), under the British Nationality Act 1981 / BOT Act 2002 classification — the British ladder's counterpart of "unincorporated territory."

So the user-prompt formulation is historically exact: Bermuda *was* administratively a Virginia colony (1612–1615), and every subsequent status it has held — Somers Isles Company colony, Crown colony, Dependent Territory, British Overseas Territory — was a reclassification imposed by external instrument, charter by charter.

## 3. The CARICOM ladder: Puerto Rico the observer, Bermuda the associate

CARICOM (Treaty of Chaguaramas, in force Aug. 1, 1973; revised 2001) runs a three-tier membership classification. **As of 2026: 15 full members, 8 associate members, 7 observers.**

| Tier | Rights | Who (2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Full member** | Voice + vote; CSME participation | Antigua & Barbuda, Bahamas, Barbados, Belize, Dominica, Grenada, Guyana, Haiti, Jamaica, Montserrat, St. Kitts & Nevis, St. Lucia, St. Vincent & Grenadines, Suriname, Trinidad & Tobago |
| **Associate member** | Participation without full rights; role formally "not yet established" in the treaty | Anguilla (1999), **Bermuda (July 2, 2003)**, BVI (1991), Cayman (2002), Turks & Caicos (1991), Curaçao, Martinique, French Guiana |
| **Observer** | Participation in technical committees | Aruba, Colombia, Dominican Republic, Mexico, **Puerto Rico**, Sint Maarten, Venezuela |

The prompt's two claims, checked:

- **"Puerto Rico is a neutral observer to CARICOM" — ✅ confirmed** (observer tier; engagement limited to technical committees; "neutral" is apt in that observers take no votes and no treaty obligations). Note the structural irony: Puerto Rico's *external-relations capacity* is itself limited by its organic-act classification (§ 1) — the U.S. federal government holds the foreign-affairs power, so observer is effectively the ceiling absent federal action.
- **"Bermuda is something else" — ✅ confirmed, and the something else is specific:** Bermuda is an **Associate Member** (since July 2, 2003, the fifth associate), having *upgraded from observer* after Premier Jennifer Smith's 2002 application and public consultation. And the ladder is in motion again: Bermuda's government has formally committed (2023 ministerial statement; the ongoing "Together for CARICOM" campaign) to pursue **full membership** — a 2016 UK "entrustment" letter authorizes Bermuda to negotiate with regional organizations, the Premier has raised full membership with the UK, and the CARICOM Legal Affairs Committee has been reviewing the candidacy. If completed, Bermuda would be the first associate ever to climb to full membership — i.e., a live jurisdictional reclassification proceeding, structurally parallel to the DEA docket tracked in the 2026-10-05 package.
