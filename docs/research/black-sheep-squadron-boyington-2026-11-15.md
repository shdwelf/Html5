# Black Sheep Squadron / Gregory “Pappy” Boyington — research continuation (2026-11-15)

## Evidence-first findings

- The U.S. Marine Corps' current VMFA-214 unit history says 27 pilots were
  brought together in August 1943 under Major Gregory Boyington to form the
  original Black Sheep; the squadron name reflected the unusual mix of
  veterans and replacement pilots. It says the second tour ended 8 January
  1944, five days after Boyington was shot down and captured. The modern unit
  page is a retrospective institutional history, not the 1943 squadron diary.
- A Marine Corps message describes Boyington (1912–1988) as a Sioux Marine
  aviator and POW, says he took VMF-214 command in September 1943, and states
  26 enemy kills for the period recognized by the Medal of Honor citation
  (12 September 1943–3 January 1944). This is a careful way to report the
  citation's credited count, not an assertion that every later tally agrees.
- The Naval History and Heritage Command biography says he commanded the
  newly formed VMF-214 in the Central Solomons campaign, was shot down near
  Rabaul on 3 January 1944, was taken by a Japanese submarine, spent about
  twenty months as a POW, and was presented the Medal of Honor by President
  Truman in October 1945. It describes 22 more aerial victories during
  VMF-214 service and a claimed total of 28 counting his American Volunteer
  Group (AVG) service. The Marine Corps' 26 and NHHC's 28 are not silently
  collapsed: they refer to differently described totals/crediting.
- The serving squadron's history is a lineage account: the modern VMFA-214
  carries the “Black Sheep” identity but is not the original 1943 unit.
  Boyington's wartime record is not proof that every event or character in the
  1970s television drama *Baa Baa Black Sheep* (later syndicated as *Black
  Sheep Squadron*) happened as portrayed. Treat the show as dramatized
  entertainment, not a primary source for mission chronology or personnel.
