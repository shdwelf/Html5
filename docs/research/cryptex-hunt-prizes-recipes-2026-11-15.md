# Cryptex Hunt prizes, recipe puzzles, and archive trail

**Research date:** 2026-11-15

## Findings

### Noble Collection

The requested [Customer Service page](https://noblecollection.com/c/Customer-Service) is a support/contact page, not a Cryptex Hunt or contest page. The current site separately lists a licensed [Da Vinci Code Cryptex](https://noblecollection.com/Item--i-PRP-DV-5120) and a miniature Cryptex key chain; these are retail products, not evidence that Noble Collection sponsored Cryptex Hunt prizes. The site's current service information is distinct from the historical Google Da Vinci Code Quest; do not conflate any of them.

### Cryptex Hunt prizes vary by year

- The official [2022 rules](https://cryptexhunt.com/2022/) state there was no prize for finishing first. They describe two drawings: a custom Nevins Line Cryptex for eligible teams finishing by March 6 at 1 p.m. ET, and a standard five-code-ring Cryptex drawing for eligible teams finishing by March 31. The [Internet Archive community-video item](https://archive.org/details/01.2022-cryptex-hunt-intro-q-a) lists a 2022 Hunt intro/Q&A, a drawing video, and cooking/food-themed program segments. These are program/video titles, not evidence that the videos contain a recipe; the large videos were not downloaded or transcribed.
- The official [2023 page](https://cryptexhunt.com/2023/) likewise specifies two Cryptex prizes awarded by drawings, with different eligibility cutoffs (May 7 and May 31). The official [previous-hunts index](https://hunt.cryptexhunt.com/previous-hunts/) lists 2023 and 2022 as separate editions.
- The official [2025 page](https://cryptexhunt.com/2025/) says that edition had no prize other than bragging rights. Therefore “Cryptex Hunt has/has no prizes” is not a timeless statement; attach the claim to the year and the rules for that edition.
- The historical Google Da Vinci Code Quest and the Cryptex Hunt are separate events. Columbia Pictures' [2006 press release](https://www.sony.com/content/sony/en/en_us/SCA/company-news/press-releases/columbia-pictures/2006/the-da-vinci-code-quest-on-google-challenges-fans-through-online-puzzle-adventure.html) said the first 10,000 eligible challengers completing 24 puzzles would be contacted for the final challenge and that selected finalists would receive a Cryptex replica. This is not evidence of a Cryptex Hunt prize or Noble Collection sponsorship.

### Book-cover barcode start: 2019

The cover-and-barcode memory identifies the **2019 Cryptex Hunt**. The official [2019 hunt page](https://cryptexhunt.com/2019/) says the hunt was found in ClueKeeper using hunt code `CXHUNT`, then started by viewing the magazine cover. The official [2019 solution guide](https://cryptexhunt.com/2019/CH2019%20Solution%20Guide.pdf) confirms that the cover was the hunt's starting puzzle:

1. The barcode's printed numbers convert to **START HERE**, confirming that the solver is looking in the right place.
2. The barcode's wide/narrow bars encode **Morse code**; the word “MORSE” is hidden in Justin's glasses as an extra hint.
3. Decoding the bars produces **CRYPTEXHUNT.COM/CH19**, leading to a jigsaw and the next start step.
4. The jigsaw's color-accented letters spell **ENGAGE**, the code for starting the hunt in the ClueKeeper app. The app's start information then supplied the magazine PDF URL and its password, **LOCKBOX**.

So the barcode was the entry trail, but it wasn't just a standalone barcode answer: the Morse-decoded URL led to a jigsaw, which yielded the ClueKeeper start code. The official page's `CXHUNT` was the separate code to find/register the hunt.

The author now recalls figuring out the cover puzzle and, with a collaborator using the handle `psygirl601`, nearly finishing by the deadline. This remains the author's memory, not an independently verified finish or placement. The official [ClueKeeper results page](https://www.cluekeeper.com/public/hunt_leaders_old?hunt_key=ag9zfmNsdWVrZWVwZXJ3ZWJyFwsSCkNsdWVPckh1bnQYgICAnoPCqgkM) exposes a long static leaderboard. An exact-name web search and inspection of its P-alphabet rows did not locate `psygirl601`; that does not rule out collaborating under another registered account. No exact finish time or completion status is inferred, and no link between the handle and a real-world identity is established here.

The author clarifies that the near-finish memory refers to the Grand Prize race's end-of-night cutoff, not the later March 31 drawing deadline. The official rules state that the first-prize contest ran March 1–3 and that the winner would be evaluated/announced on or after March 3, but do not specify an hour in the fine print. Preserve the author's “end of the night” wording without inferring the exact date, clock time, or time zone. The hunt remained open through March 31 for the second-prize drawing. The official FAQ says each player registered and played as an individual but explicitly welcomed people solving together; the prize went to the qualifying account owner. ClueKeeper's listing describes the hunt as about eight hours with unlimited team size. These rules make collaboration compatible with the contest format, but do not independently verify the author's near-finish recollection.

### Recipe puzzle: 2025 Word Salad

The official 2025 solution PDF, [Puzzle 10: Word Salad](https://cryptexhunt.com/2025/solutions/CH2025-P10_Word_Salad_Solution.pdf), yields **OLIVIER**. The recipe clue describes a Russian salad with potato, carrot, cucumber, peas, egg, celeriac, onion, apple, and chicken. This is the hunt puzzle's recipe answer, not an official endorsed recipe with quantities or preparation steps.

## Conclusion

Cryptex Hunt awards are year-specific, not a single standing prize rule. The 2022 and 2023 editions used drawings for physical Cryptexes; the 2025 edition offered bragging rights only. The confirmed recipe-themed puzzle result is 2025's **OLIVIER** salad. Archive.org indexes food-themed video programs from the 2022 hunt media package, but their recipes/content remain unverified.
