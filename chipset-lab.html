02RTL STATUS WORD · BOOT REQUIREMENTS · ATA SECURITY

Chipset
Boot Gate

The chipset in rtl/ measures every boot stage — MBR, ESP application, second-stage loader — and refuses the handoff unless the whole chain matched, in order, with no tamper latch. This page decodes the status word it publishes and checks the requirements a UEFI or legacy boot actually needs. The layout is parsed out of the Verilog, not typed in here: tools/verify_bootchain.mjs fails if this page and the RTL disagree.

Boot requirements

ATA security gates (what hdparm can and cannot do)

The last command latched by the ATA FSM, with its hdparm equivalent and the gate that decided it. On a tampered platform every destructive command is refused by the chipset — not by the drive — which is why the refusal is latched as evidence rather than silently dropped.

What this page does and does not check

  • Checked here: the platform half — chain verified, arm latched, no tamper, lock state, policy match, media reads, CPU release, and that an untrusted platform grants nothing destructive. Those rules live in js/bootchain.js and are diffed against tools/bootchain.py on every cycle of the golden transcript.
  • Not checked here: the disk half — MBR/fdisk tables, the GPT header and both CRCs, the protective MBR, the ESP's FAT volume, the PE32+ boot application and the legacy loader. That needs a disk image, and it runs on the command line: python3 tools/bootchain.py --image disk.img --policy uefi (fixtures: --fixtures DIR, verification: --selftest).
  • Where the numbers come from: status-field offsets and the DENY/ABORT/LCK constants are read out of rtl/vchip_top.v and rtl/vchip_pkg.vh by python3 tools/bootchain.py --layout-json. Nothing on this page is a transcription.
