# Jim Sanborn / installation source check

**Research pass:** 2026-10-02 (America/Puerto Rico)

## Scope

This pass normalizes the name to **Jim Sanborn** (Herbert James Sanborn, Jr.) and separates primary/first-person evidence from secondary catalogues. The new `sanborn-restaurant-4dwm.html` is an **original design study**, not a claim that Jim Sanborn designed the room shown in the viewer.

The phrases “blueprint,” “wiremesh,” and “livelight” are treated as design directions, not as a documented title or construction specification. The viewer uses them as three inspectable layers:

- **blueprint** — plan datum, grid, room outline, and source-readable dimensions;
- **wiremesh** — a visual study layer for the booth shell, rails, and edges;
- **livelight** — interactive point lights and emissive rails, echoing Sanborn’s documented use of projection and pinpoint light without pretending to reproduce an undocumented fixture.

## Official-site coverage map

The official site presents works under several groups. This is a research index, not a claim that every work has a surviving public blueprint.

- **Science-based installations:** `Critical Assembly` (1998–2004, Gwangju Biennale / later National Museum of Nuclear Science & History collection) and `Terrestrial Physics` (2010, MCA Denver / later Intellectual Ventures collection); the site describes original parts, artifacts, drawings, photographs, and reconstructed machines.
- **Installation archives:** `Covert Obsolescence: The Code Room` (1993), `Animisme` (1993), `Thunderhead Horizon` (1985), `The Mummy Room` (1980), `All Things Turned to Stone` (1988), `Invisible Forces` (1985), `North by Northwest` (1981), `Covert Obsolescence: The Listening Post` (1993, studio and Corcoran versions), `Coriolis` (1985–1992), and `Quadrant` (1999).
- **Landscape projections:** `Bandon, Oregon II` (1997), `Horse Valley, Utah IV` (1995), `Cainville, Utah` (1995), `Rough Rock, Arizona` (1996), `Kilkee County Claire, Ireland` (1997), `Notom, Utah` (1995), `Green River, Utah` (1997), and `Longsturn County Cork, Ireland` (1997). The official description says the projector and camera were typically about one-half mile from the subject landscape and that the works were photographed at night.
- **Public spaces:** `Radiance` (2008), `Lux` (2001), `Kryptos` (1989), `Coastline` (1993), `Paleos` (1994), `Rippawam` (1999), `A Comma, A` (2004), `Antipodes` (1997), `Argentum: Double-Positive`, `Meridian`, and `Bright Stars Memorial` (completed 2023). The official descriptions repeatedly specify bronze or copper, waterjet-cut or encoded text, light / projection cylinders, stone, and site-specific context.
- **New work / geometry:** the official site currently presents `Archeo-Geometry 2021–2025`, including Euclidean and Cartesian pieces in bronze.
- **Archives and image bodies:** the site also presents `Babylon`, `Central Asia`, `Archeotranscription`, `Covert Obsolescence`, `Clandestine Device`, and related etched copper / pulped-document works.

**Primary index:** [Jim Sanborn — main / installations](https://jimsanborn.net/main.html). Because the site is image-heavy and its archive is not a formal database, names are transcribed as displayed and should be checked against the linked image/catalogue when exact spelling or dimensions matter.

## Source notes

### 1. Official catalogue — highest authority for titles and placement

The artist’s site lists public-space and installation-archive works, including materials and dimensions for `Kryptos`, `Coastline`, `Critical Assembly`, `Terrestrial Physics`, `Invisible Forces`, and `North by Northwest`. The site is the right authority for what it chooses to publish about the artist’s own work, but it should not be stretched to prove details it does not state.

**Use in project:** metadata banner and research panel; no invented restaurant attribution.

### 2. Smithsonian Archives of American Art oral history — process and light

Sanborn describes early large stone installations, lodestones, compass needles, pinpoint bulbs, sharp shadows, and the way he researches and develops a model before fabrication. This supports an interpretive language of precise light, material evidence, and hidden information. It does not describe a wiremesh restaurant.

**Use in project:** blueprint/wiremesh/livelight layer labels are explicitly marked as a design translation.

### 3. Atomic Heritage Foundation / National Museum of Nuclear Science & History — blueprint and cable vocabulary

The interview transcript records Sanborn’s account of `Atomic Time`: he acquired original equipment and artifacts, worked from blueprints, used period lamps, and organized cables and sound in a dark lab-like environment. This is direct testimony and is useful for atmosphere and research method, not for restaurant dimensions.

### 4. Lafayette exhibition research — cross-check

The exhibition page summarizes `Kryptos`, the 1995–1998 `Topographic Projection and Implied Geometry` series, `Atomic Time`, `Terrestrial Physics`, and `Without Provenance`. It is a useful institutional cross-check for dates and project descriptions, but remains an exhibition page rather than a fabrication drawing.

### 5. Secondary list — useful lead, not a blueprint

Elonka Dunin’s older selected-works list is valuable as a historical index and clearly labels itself as a fan resource elsewhere on her site. It is the lead for the `Zola Spy Restaurant` name, but its `??` fields and lack of a primary plan mean the restaurant reference remains **needs confirmation**. The viewer avoids presenting its geometry as the original work.

## Implementation boundary

`sanborn-restaurant.wrl` is valid VRML97 plus `XDC_*` comment directives consumed by the HTML5 viewer. The directives make the geometry inspectable and source-readable. The files intentionally distinguish:

1. **documented research** — source cards and notes above;
2. **viewer mechanics** — Three.js, OrbitControls, WebGL, VRML manifest parser;
3. **design extrapolation** — booths, stools, cashier, flip-up pass, glass case, and deep-fry kitchen.

That separation is the source-check: the visual study can be useful without being misrepresented as a recovered Sanborn plan.
